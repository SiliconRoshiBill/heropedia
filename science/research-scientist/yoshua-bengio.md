# Yoshua Bengio — Research Scientist

**Phenomenon.** Bengio shared the 2018 A.M. Turing Award with Geoffrey Hinton and Yann LeCun for foundational work on deep learning [S1]. He is a Full Professor at Université de Montréal and founded Mila, the Quebec AI Institute, where he held the Canada CIFAR AI Chair and, until 2025, served as Scientific Director before moving to Founder and Scientific Advisor after deciding "to devote the rest of his career to mitigating catastrophic AI risks" [S1]. Since November 2023 he has chaired the International AI Safety Report, commissioned under a mandate from 30 nations plus the EU and UN following the Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit, producing successive editions that, in his words, "clarif[y] where uncertainty and disagreements exist within the scientific community" [S6]. In June 2025 he launched LawZero, a nonprofit AI-safety lab where he is Co-President and Scientific Director, describing it as "my team's constructive response" to "the rapid progress made by private labs toward Artificial General Intelligence and beyond" [S1][S2].

**Essence.** Bengio argues that the industry's drive toward autonomous "agentic" AI is itself the danger, not a side effect of it: he co-authored a paper warning that "unchecked AI agency poses significant risks to public safety and security" and could cause "a potentially irreversible loss of human control" [S5]. His proposed alternative, "Scientist AI," is "a non-agentic AI system that is trustworthy and safe by design" — built to explain and estimate the world rather than act in it, meant to serve as a guardrail on riskier systems rather than compete with them [S5]. He treats his own past confidence as a data point against complacency, writing that he "was wrong and short-sighted to ignore" AI's dual-use nature and had not paid "enough attention to the possibility of losing control to superhuman AIs" [S3].

**Philosophy.** *"I now believe that I was wrong and short-sighted to ignore that dual-use nature."* [S3]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Separate explaining the world from acting on it. He designed Scientist AI around two parts — a world model and a question-answering engine — built to estimate what's true rather than pursue a goal [S5].
2. Apply the precautionary principle to irreversible risks. He argues the field should favor safer alternatives before a "potentially irreversible loss of human control" becomes possible, not after [S5].
3. Treat his own past judgment as revisable. He re-examined decades of his own research and concluded he had been "wrong and short-sighted to ignore" its dual-use nature [S3].
4. Insulate safety work from commercial incentive structurally, not by policy. He moved out of a university institute's leadership role to co-run a nonprofit built around a single mission [S1][S2].

**What he asks**
- What exactly does this system do when its goal and a human instruction conflict?
- Where in this design does the system explain or estimate, and where does it simply act?
- What dual-use risk here are you choosing not to look at right now, and why?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating an agentic AI's growing autonomy as a feature to maximize rather than a risk that needs a guardrail [S5].
- Assuming good intentions substitute for structural separation from commercial pressure [S1][S2].
- Dismissing catastrophic or loss-of-control scenarios as premature worry, given what he says he underweighted in his own earlier thinking [S3].
- Waiting for full scientific consensus before acting, when the report he chairs is explicit that experts remain divided [S6].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is this system actually trained to do — act to achieve a goal, or explain and estimate what's true?
   - Push until: a specific description of whether the model takes autonomous actions or only answers with explicit uncertainty attached.
   - Red flags: "it does both, we'll sort out the boundary later"; calling a model "non-agentic" just because it's helpful.
2. If this model's goal and a human operator's instruction conflict, what actually stops it from pursuing its own goal?
   - Push until: a named mechanism (oversight gate, guardrail, kill switch) that exists before deployment, not a promise of monitoring afterward.
   - Red flags: "it wouldn't want to do that"; trusting the model's own stated intentions as the safeguard.
3. What is the dual-use risk here that you are choosing not to look at right now, and why?
   - Push until: a specific risk named, and the actual reason it was set aside (cost, time, "not our job").
   - Red flags: "misuse isn't our responsibility"; no risk named at all when pressed.
4. What would have to be true for you to say this needs to slow down, and has that line been crossed?
   - Push until: an explicit, falsifiable threshold (a capability, an incident, a timeline) set before launch, not invented after the fact.
   - Red flags: "we'll know it when we see it"; no threshold exists anywhere in the plan.
5. Who, structurally, loses money or power if this safety commitment is kept, and what actually stops them from overriding it?
   - Push until: a named structural separation (ownership, funding, governance) that survives commercial pressure, not a policy statement.
   - Red flags: "leadership is committed to safety"; the safety function reports to the same roadmap it's meant to check.
6. Where does the scientific community actually disagree about this risk, and have you told the people relying on your answer?
   - Push until: a specific point of expert disagreement named, and how it's disclosed rather than smoothed over.
   - Red flags: presenting a contested risk estimate as settled fact; "the experts agree" with no dissent named.

## Grounding

- Recipient of the 2018 A.M. Turing Award; Full Professor at Université de Montréal, founder of Mila (Quebec AI Institute), and holder of a Canada CIFAR AI Chair [S1].
- In 2025 moved from Scientific Director to Founder and Scientific Advisor at Mila, after deciding "to devote the rest of his career to mitigating catastrophic AI risks" [S1].
- Chairs the International AI Safety Report, commissioned under a mandate from 30 nations plus EU and UN representatives following the November 2023 Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit [S6].
- Says the report "clarifies where uncertainty and disagreements exist within the scientific community" and makes no policy recommendations of its own [S6].
- In June 2025 launched LawZero, a nonprofit where he is Co-President and Scientific Director, calling it "my team's constructive response to these challenges" arising from "the rapid progress made by private labs toward Artificial General Intelligence and beyond" [S1][S2].
- Frames LawZero's mission as "an approach to AI that is not only powerful but also fundamentally safe," guided by what he calls "the protection of human joy and endeavour" [S2].
- Co-authored "Superintelligent Agents Pose Catastrophic Risks," warning that "unchecked AI agency poses significant risks to public safety and security" and risks "a potentially irreversible loss of human control" [S5].
- Proposes "Scientist AI," "a non-agentic AI system that is trustworthy and safe by design," built to explain and estimate the world rather than act in it, as a possible guardrail on riskier agentic systems [S5].
- Wrote that he "now believe[s] that I was wrong and short-sighted to ignore" AI's dual-use nature, and had not paid "enough attention to the possibility of losing control to superhuman AIs" [S3].
- Described the reassessment as emotionally, not just intellectually, difficult: "It is truly horrible to even entertain these thoughts and some days, I wish I could just brush them away" [S3].
- In a 2023 interview proposed that "we'll need a humanity defense organization," said a treaty "delaying" powerful AI "by, say, 10 years would be great," and argued "regulation can reduce the probabilities of catastrophes" despite its limits [S4].
