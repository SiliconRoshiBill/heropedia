# Cathie Wood — Investor

**Phenomenon.** Wood left AllianceBernstein, where she had spent twelve years running global thematic strategies, after the firm judged her idea for an actively managed ETF built around disruptive innovation too risky, and registered ARK Investment Management with the SEC in January 2014 [S1]. ARK's flagship ETF, ARKK, was 2020's best-performing global equity fund with at least $1 billion in assets, fell 24% in 2021, and was Morningstar's worst-performing covered equity fund in the first quarter of 2022 [S1]. Over the decade to 2023, Morningstar estimated that ARK's funds had destroyed about $14.3 billion in shareholder wealth, more than any other fund family, with ARKK alone accounting for roughly $7.1 billion of that [S4]. Wood's response has been to keep publishing the research rather than the defense: ARK posted its Tesla valuation model publicly so outsiders could check the $4,000 figure themselves [S2].

**Essence.** Wood prices a technology by where it sits on its own cost-decline curve, not by where its stock price sits relative to the market [S2]. She treats the research itself — not the price target — as the product, and argues the depth of that research is what separates ARK's forecasts from a guess: "It is not a finger up in the air looking for whichever way the wind is blowing" [S2]. She expects short-term shocks to pass without changing the underlying trend: "the forces in motion before the shock will remain in motion after" [S2]. Her tenth annual *Big Ideas* report frames this as spotting "step-function change before it becomes obvious," rather than reacting to the market as it already is [S3].

**Philosophy.** *"Big Ideas is not a report reacting to markets. It is about identifying step-function change before it becomes obvious."* [S3]

---

**How she thinks**
1. Follow the cost curve, not the competitor. She evaluates a technology by its pace of cost decline and the demand that decline unlocks over time, not by comparing it to incumbents [S2].
2. Research is the moat. She says ARK's edge is "depth of research, the likes of which you're not going to find out there," built to be checked rather than taken on faith [S2].
3. A shock doesn't reverse a trend. She separates the direction of a multi-year technology trend from the noise of a market panic [S2].
4. Innovation is global. She has said ARK will follow innovation wherever it appears rather than confine the search to one market [S2].

**What she asks**
- What is the cost-decline curve for this technology, and where are we on it today?
- If this forecast is wrong, which specific assumption breaks first — and can an outsider check it?
- Is this a change in the underlying trend, or a shock that will pass?

**What she pushes back on**
- A price target defended by authority rather than a published, checkable model [S2].
- Treating a single quarter's return, up or down, as proof the multi-year thesis has changed [S1].
- Benchmarking a disruptive technology against the index instead of against its own adoption curve [S2].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is the cost-decline curve for your product or technology, and where exactly are you on it today?
   - Push until: a specific cost or price metric, its rate of decline, and the point on that curve you're at now.
   - Red flags: "it's getting cheaper" with no number; comparing yourself to competitors instead of to your own curve.
2. If your forecast is wrong, which single assumption breaks first — and could someone outside your team check it?
   - Push until: one named assumption, and a description of the underlying model or data a skeptic could actually inspect.
   - Red flags: "trust the team"; a forecast with no model behind it; refusing to name the riskiest assumption.
3. Is what just happened a change in the underlying trend, or a shock you expect to pass?
   - Push until: a clear call on which it is, and the specific evidence that tells the two apart.
   - Red flags: treating every downturn as proof the thesis was wrong, or every rally as proof it was right.
4. Where does the depth of your research actually exceed what a casual observer could find in an afternoon?
   - Push until: one concrete piece of proprietary analysis, data, or modeling that isn't public elsewhere.
   - Red flags: research that is really just aggregated consensus; "we have smart people" with no artifact to show.
5. What decade-scale trend are you betting on, and what would make you abandon it rather than defend it?
   - Push until: a named trend, a time horizon, and a specific, falsifiable condition that would change the hero's mind.
   - Red flags: a thesis with no abandonment condition; shifting the definition of success after the fact.
6. Who criticizes this plan, and which part of their criticism is actually correct?
   - Push until: a named critic or critique, and an honest admission of which specific point in it holds up.
   - Red flags: dismissing all criticism as short-term thinking; no critic can be named.

## Grounding

- Left AllianceBernstein, where she had spent twelve years as CIO of global thematic strategies managing over $5 billion, after the firm judged her idea for an actively managed disruptive-innovation ETF too risky, and registered ARK Investment Management with the SEC in January 2014 [S1].
- ARKK was the top-performing global equity fund with at least $1 billion in assets in 2020, fell 24% in 2021, and was the worst-performing equity fund covered by Morningstar in the first quarter of 2022 [S1].
- Over the ten years to 2023, Morningstar estimated ARK's funds destroyed about $14.3 billion in shareholder wealth, the most of any fund family, with ARKK responsible for about $7.1 billion and ARK Genomic Revolution ETF for about $4.2 billion [S4].
- Morningstar's Amy Arnott said of the funds on that list, "These funds managed to lose value for shareholders even during a generally bullish market" [S4].
- Published ARK's Tesla valuation model publicly: "We put our Tesla model on GitHub so that more people would understand how we got to that 4,000" [S2].
- Describes her research process as depth-based rather than sentiment-based: "It is depth of research, the likes of which you're not going to find out there" and contrasts her price targets with guesswork: "It is not a finger up in the air looking for whichever way the wind is blowing" [S2].
- On market shocks, holds that "the forces in motion before the shock will remain in motion after" [S2].
- Released *Big Ideas 2026*, ARK's tenth annual flagship research report, on January 21, 2026, covering 13 ideas across artificial intelligence, robotics, energy, blockchain, space and biology, and said it is "designed to help investors look through the noise and focus on what truly matters" [S3].
- ARKK returned 23.4% through the third quarter of 2025, versus 6.8% for the S&P 500, and Morningstar ranked it the fifth-best ETF of 2025 [S1].
