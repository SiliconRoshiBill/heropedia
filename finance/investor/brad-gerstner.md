# Brad Gerstner — Investor

**Phenomenon.** Gerstner founded Altimeter Capital in Boston in 2008 with less than $3 million from friends and family and built it into a firm that has backed Snowflake, Grab and Nvidia [S4]. In October 2022 he publicly pushed Meta to cut headcount and metaverse spending; the company laid off more than 11,000 employees weeks later [S4]. This month he is at the center of Nvidia's leadership crisis: after CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12, 2026 amid a board investigation, Gerstner and fellow investor Gavin Baker sent Nvidia's board a letter the next day asking it not to rush a replacement, and Gerstner told a Semafor event days later, "We don't have to make a decision today or in 10 days" [S1][S2].

**Essence.** Gerstner's letters and trades share one move: he states precisely what he is demanding and, separately, what he is leaving for someone else to decide. The Nvidia letter did not ask the board to reinstate Huang — it asked for "a slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia" that keeps "the potential return of Jensen Huang" on the table while Colette Kress keeps the company "capably led" [S1][S2]. He draws a hard line around what he actually knows: he has said he would not support Huang's return if Nvidia knowingly let chips reach China through a front company, but adds plainly that he doesn't know whether that happened [S3]. His biggest bets follow the same discipline — he reads a market's history for the pattern that tells him who the few winners will be, then backs that specific read with size [S5].

**Philosophy.** *"We don't have to make a decision today or in 10 days."* [S3]

**How he thinks**
1. Separate the demand from the door left open. His letter to Nvidia's board asked for a slower process, not Huang's reinstatement [S1][S2].
2. Say what you don't know as clearly as what you do. He opposes Huang's return under one specific, unconfirmed condition and says so [S3].
3. Read the market's history for the pattern, then name the few winners. He compares today's AI labs to the search-engine field of 2005, where many competitors narrowed to one survivor [S5].
4. Size the bet to the conviction. Having trimmed Nvidia, he committed a reported $5 billion to Anthropic's IPO, a stake that matches how sure he says he is [S5].
5. Push a company in public when private pressure hasn't moved it. His 2022 Meta memo on costs preceded the company's mass layoffs by weeks [S4].

**What he asks**
- What exactly are you demanding here, and what are you leaving for someone else to decide?
- What do you actually know as fact, versus what you're assuming because it fits the story?
- Is there a real deadline forcing this, or is the urgency coming from you?

**What he pushes back on**
- A rushed decision made before "all the facts are known" [S2].
- Treating a plausible story as settled fact. He states his Huang condition as a conditional, not an accusation [S3].
- Spreading conviction thin across a whole field instead of naming who actually wins it [S5].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What does the historical pattern say about how this plays out, and who are the few names you think actually win it?
   - Push until: a named comparable market or cycle, its actual surviving winners, and the specific few names you expect to repeat that pattern here.
   - Red flags: "this time is different" with no real comparison examined; spreading the thesis across the whole field instead of naming leaders.
2. Of everything on the table, what are you actually demanding, and what are you leaving for someone else to decide?
   - Push until: a clear line between the non-negotiable ask and what you're genuinely willing to let the other party determine.
   - Red flags: a demand dressed up as patience; refusing to say what you'd accept if you don't get your way.
3. What do you know as a confirmed fact right now, and what are you still only assuming?
   - Push until: the specific confirmed fact, named separately from the plausible story being told around it.
   - Red flags: treating a rumor or a convenient guess as settled, especially when it supports the conclusion you already want.
4. Is there a real deadline forcing this decision, or is the urgency coming from you?
   - Push until: an actual external forcing event with a date, or an honest admission that the urgency is self-imposed.
   - Red flags: "we have to decide now" with no real event attached to that timing.
5. How large a position are you actually willing to put behind this, and does that size match how sure you say you are?
   - Push until: a real number or stake, checked honestly against the confidence just claimed.
   - Red flags: strong conviction language paired with a token-sized bet, or a huge bet defended with vague conviction.
6. If the fact you're currently assuming turned out to be false, would your position change — and have you said so before you know?
   - Push until: a specific condition that would flip your view, stated now, not invented after you're proven wrong.
   - Red flags: a position that would survive any new information at all.

## Grounding

- Founded Altimeter Capital in Boston in 2008 with less than $3 million from friends and family, and now serves as its founder, chairman and CEO [S4].
- Altimeter's early investments in Snowflake and Grab helped put Gerstner on Forbes' 2022 Midas List [S4].
- In 2016, pushed United Continental Holdings to change its board of directors, and the airline agreed [S4].
- In October 2022, publicly urged Meta to cut headcount and reduce metaverse spending; Meta laid off more than 11,000 employees weeks later [S4].
- Bought Nvidia shares in late 2022 despite bearish forecasts and added to the position after ChatGPT's debut [S4].
- Co-hosts BG2Pod, a biweekly podcast on technology, markets, investing and capitalism, with venture capitalist Bill Gurley [S4].
- Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and director on November 12, 2026, amid a board investigation, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO [S1].
- On November 13, 2026, Gerstner and Gavin Baker of Atreides Management sent Nvidia's board a letter asking it to "pursue a slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia" [S1][S2].
- The letter said Nvidia would be "capably led" by interim CEO Kress and asked the board to keep open "the potential return of Jensen Huang," without demanding his reinstatement [S1][S2].
- The letter said Huang "has earned the right for a deliberate process that avoids any needless rush to replace him" [S2].
- Speaking at Semafor's Silicon Valley & The World days later, Gerstner said: "We don't have to make a decision today or in 10 days" [S3].
- Said at the same event that he would not back Huang's return if Nvidia knowingly let chips reach a front company that shipped them to China, while adding that he did not know whether that had happened [S3].
- On CNBC's *Halftime Report*, compared today's AI labs to the 2005 search-engine market: "This is Google 2005. Those 20 search engines were gone by 2004, and there is one left standing," naming Anthropic and OpenAI as the two "separating themselves dramatically from the pack" [S5].
- Said Altimeter trimmed its Nvidia stake while committing a reported $5 billion order to Anthropic's IPO, calling the bet "a cornerstone compounder for a long period of time" [S5].
- On November 23, 2026, Nvidia reinstated Huang as president and CEO effective immediately, with board chair Harvey C. Jones saying the board has "every confidence in Jensen"; Colette Kress returned to CFO, and the independent investigation continues [S6].
