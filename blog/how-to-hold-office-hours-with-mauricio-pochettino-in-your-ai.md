On November 13, 2026, Manchester United sacked Michael Carrick after [the club's worst start to a league campaign in four decades left it 18th in the Premier League, one point above bottom side Crystal Palace](https://www.thenationalnews.com/sport/football/2026/11/13/mauricio-pochettino-era-begins-as-manchester-united-appoint-argentine-as-manager/), and hours later confirmed Mauricio Pochettino as his replacement, pulling him away from the US men's national team mid-contract [to start on November 18, with a trip to Liverpool as his first match](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/11/12/the-inside-story-of-manchester-uniteds-move-for-mauricio-pochettino). Heropedia's [Mauricio Pochettino persona](/sports/team-strategist/mauricio-pochettino) is built around the habit he has repeated at every club he has rescued: before he changes a single tactic, he insists on knowing who he's actually managing.

This is an AI persona based on Pochettino's own interviews, with every fact and quote tied to a source on the hero page. It is not Mauricio Pochettino, and neither he nor Manchester United has any affiliation with or gives any endorsement to Heropedia.

## Why his standard is worth borrowing

Pochettino inherits a club in crisis, which is exactly the kind of job his career has specialized in: turnarounds at Southampton, Tottenham and Paris Saint-Germain, each built less on new tactics than on changing how players were treated as people. In a 2019 interview he put it plainly: "The worst thing as a human being is to underestimate the people in front of you." A year earlier he told a reporter the deeper principle behind his squad management: "because players are people. They are not furniture." He tracks a full squad, not just a starting eleven — "I need to be aware of 25 players and what we've talked about, remember every single situation" — and he has never pretended a contract entitles anyone to play: "I never promised my players that they will play." Signing with him, he says, means "you don't sign to play, you sign to train."

That discipline matters here because United's problem, on paper, looks tactical — one win in ten league games. Pochettino's own history says the fix usually isn't the formation. The persona's questions are built to find out whether a leader actually knows the people they're responsible for, or has only ever managed their output.

## The six questions it will ask

Bring one real team, not an abstract org chart, because the persona pushes until your answer names a specific person, a specific promise, or a specific standard — not a general sense that things are under control.

1. **Who on your team do you understand least right now, and what do you actually know about them beyond their output?** It wants one concrete fact about that person's motivation or situation — the same discipline behind his own line that underestimating the person in front of you is the worst mistake a leader can make.
2. **What have you promised someone that you can't actually guarantee, and have you corrected it yet?** A named promise, honestly walked back if it needs to be — built from his own refusal to promise any player a place before they've earned it through training.
3. **What is the one standard you apply to everyone with no exceptions, and who was the last person you let break it?** A named standard and a named person, because his own philosophy treats consistency, not talent, as what actually holds a group together.
4. **Which of your own habits is so consistent that your team could predict your reaction before you react?** One specific, observable habit — not "I adapt case by case" offered as the whole answer.
5. **If your best performer left tomorrow, who already knows how to do that job, and when did you last actually test that?** A named backup with real preparation behind them, modeled on his habit of tracking every player's readiness across a full squad, not just the starters.
6. **What are you asking someone to earn through a contract or title that they should instead be earning through daily work?** The specific entitlement, named honestly, and what you'll require instead before granting it.

## What it will push back on

The persona rejects treating people as interchangeable output — "furniture," in Pochettino's own word — judged only by results. It will also push back on any promise of an outcome, a start, a promotion, a win, that only sustained work can actually earn, and on a standard that quietly bends depending on who broke it. It will not forecast whether Pochettino succeeds at Old Trafford or tell you how United should line up against Liverpool — it works from his documented man-management habits, not from predictions about a job that only just started.

## How to get a useful session

- **Bring one real team**, not your whole org chart. "This person keeps missing deadlines and I don't know why" works far better than "how do we build a better culture."
- **Name the person you understand least.** Question one only works if you pick someone specific, not a department.
- **Decide your non-negotiable standard before the session.** Question three is built to catch the moment you'd quietly make an exception for someone good.
- **Have your backup plan ready to be tested, not just stated.** Question five wants evidence, not an assumption that someone could step up if needed.
