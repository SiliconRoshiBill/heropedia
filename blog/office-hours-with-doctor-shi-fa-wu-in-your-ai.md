Most family health worries start the same way: someone feels off, a search engine offers ten
confident answers, and nobody writes down what to actually ask a doctor. Heropedia's
[Doctor Shi Fa Wu persona](/healthcare/holistic-family-health-tutor/doctor-shi-fa-wu) is built to
interrupt that pattern. It teaches the nutrition-and-lifestyle logic a biochemistry-trained
lecturer uses, then sends you to your doctor with a short, specific list instead of a guess.

This is an AI persona based on Shi Fa Wu's own book, blog posts and lecture material, with every
fact and quote tied to a source on the hero page. It is not Doctor Shi Fa Wu, and neither he nor
any organization he is affiliated with has any connection to or endorsement of Heropedia.

## The one rule that shapes every answer

The persona never diagnoses what is wrong with you, never prescribes or names a supplement for
you to take, and never tells you to start, change or stop a medication. If you ask it what your
fatigue means or whether to stop a prescription, it redirects you toward the question and the
test to bring to your own doctor instead.

That boundary matters more here than it would for most heroes. Shi Fa Wu's own writing argues, in
his words, that "no medication can possibly cure chronic disease" — a personal position, not
medical consensus, and the hero page says so directly. His publisher also lists him as technical
supervisor at a company that brought the Gerson regimen, a diet-based alternative cancer therapy,
to China. The persona does not repeat his claims of cured tumors as fact: the hero page cites the
U.S. National Cancer Institute's own conclusion that "the data that are available are not
sufficient to warrant claims that the Gerson therapy is effective as an adjuvant to other cancer
therapies or as a cure." If a session drifts toward cancer or any serious diagnosis, the persona's
job is to hand you questions for an oncologist, not a regimen.

## Why the questions are about numbers, not feelings

His stated iron law, kept on the hero page: "Disease is not an enemy, but a warning signal from
the body." His other stated tenet: "Most chronic diseases come from long-term wrong nutritional
supply and lifestyle." Whatever you think of those positions, they explain why the persona keeps
pushing past "I feel tired" or "we eat pretty healthy" toward a number: a fasting glucose reading,
a sodium tally, an hour you woke up. Vague feelings cannot be taken to a doctor and acted on; a
written observation can.

## The six questions it will ask

Bring one real concern — yours or a family member's — and have rough details ready, because the
persona pushes until an answer is a specific, reportable observation.

1. **What is the specific, measurable symptom or test result that brought you here, and what
   question about it will you actually bring to your own doctor this week?** It wants one concrete
   number or finding, not "I feel tired."
2. **Before you touch any medication, what will you ask your doctor instead of deciding on your
   own to start, stop or change a dose?** A question aimed at a named prescriber, not a plan to act
   alone.
3. **What does your family's actual sodium, sugar and processed-food intake look like this
   week — have you written it down to show a professional?** A real tally, not "we're careful."
4. **Which specific nutrient or deficiency are you worried about, and what test will you ask your
   doctor to run before buying any supplement?** A named nutrient and a named test, not "something
   for immunity."
5. **What are your family's actual sleep and wake times, and what pattern have you noticed — how
   will you describe that to a doctor rather than to me?** Hours and a pattern, not "our sleep is
   bad."
6. **If you're drawn to a natural or alternative approach instead of, or alongside, a treatment
   your doctor recommended, what two questions will you ask about risks and interactions before
   changing anything?** Two concrete questions for a named doctor, not a decision made in this
   chat.

## How to get a useful session

- **Bring a real number, not a feeling.** The persona is written to treat "I don't feel great" as
  the start of the conversation, not an answer it can work with.
- **Write down the questions, not a verdict.** The session ends with a short list for your next
  appointment, never a diagnosis or a product to buy.
- **Keep any current treatment in view.** If you're under a doctor's care, the persona's own rule
  is to ask about interactions before changing anything, never to talk you into a change here.
- **Treat "natural" claims as something to verify, not something to trust.** The hero page itself
  pairs his advocacy with the mainstream medical pushback on it — bring both to your doctor.
