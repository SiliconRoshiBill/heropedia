On November 18 and 19, Xi Jinping is set to sit down with Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen for [what the White House is calling the first three-way meeting ever held among the three leaders](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/trump-xi-and-putin-to-hold-first-three-way-summit-in-china). Nuclear-arms stockpiles and the Iran war are reportedly on the agenda. It is the kind of moment that makes Xi's own, very consistent rhetorical habit worth studying: for nearly a decade, in speech after speech, he has described every major initiative — globalization, the Belt and Road, trade — as "win-win cooperation," while quietly telling his own Party to build the self-reliance to survive losing access to any of it. Heropedia's [Xi Jinping persona](/politics/president/xi-jinping) is built around that exact tension, and it will not let you claim a "win-win" without naming what the other side gets.

This is an AI persona based on Xi's own speeches and official Party documents, with every fact and quote tied to a source on the hero page. It is not Xi Jinping, and neither he nor the Chinese government has any affiliation with or gives any endorsement to Heropedia.

## Why "win-win" is the word to interrogate

At Davos in January 2017, Xi told the World Economic Forum that "the global economy is the big ocean that you cannot escape from" and warned that "pursuing protectionism is like locking oneself in a dark room." Six years later, at the third Belt and Road Forum in October 2023, he put the same idea more directly: "win-win cooperation is the sure way to success in launching major initiatives that benefit all," and "ideological confrontation, geopolitical rivalry and bloc politics are not a choice for us."

That framing sounds generous until you notice what runs alongside it. His report to the 20th Party Congress, approved in October 2022, called for China to "achieve greater self-reliance and strength in science and technology" and told the Party to "be more mindful of potential dangers, be prepared to deal with worst-case scenarios." The persona's first move is to hold both statements to account at once: if you are promising someone a win, can you say — in terms they would recognize — what they actually get? And if you are building insurance against losing access to something, have you actually started, or is it still a plan?

## The six questions it will ask

Bring one real deal, partnership or dependency, not an abstract strategy, because the persona wants a specific answer each time.

1. **If you call this deal a "win-win," what does the other side get that they themselves would recognize as a win — not what you assume they want?** It wants a benefit stated in the other party's own terms, not your framing of their interest.
2. **What is the one input, supplier or platform this plan depends on that you do not control, and what have you built to survive losing it?** A named dependency plus a funded, dated alternative — not a hope that it won't happen.
3. **Looking ten years out rather than one, what does this plan need to be self-reliant on, and what is the first step you have already taken toward that?** One completed or budgeted step, not just a forward-looking promise.
4. **What is the worst case for this plan, and what have you actually set aside to survive it, versus just watching for it?** A specific reserve or fallback with a number attached.
5. **Where are you calling a disagreement a "win-win" when, in the other party's own words, they describe it differently?** The persona wants you to go find and read their actual stated position, not assume it.
6. **If you cut this plan off from the outside entirely, what do you actually lose that staying open was giving you?** A concrete, named loss — a customer, a technology, a price — not a vague fear of isolation.

## What it will push back on

The persona rejects treating a trade or technology dispute as a fight only one side can win. Xi's own line from Davos is blunt on this: "no one will emerge as a winner in a trade war." It will also reject protectionism offered as a safe default — in his framing, the closed door costs you more than it protects — and it will not let you declare victory or defeat before the long game has actually played out.

The persona does not take a position on Taiwan, the war in Ukraine, or any other live dispute Xi is party to, and it will not tell you how to vote or who is right in a geopolitical argument. Where the hero page documents criticism of his record — including [a 2022 UN-linked report finding that China's detention of Uyghur and other Muslim groups in Xinjiang may constitute crimes against humanity](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/08/31/china-new-un-report-alleges-crimes-against-humanity) — it states that fact and China's rejection of it side by side, as sourced record, not as an argument either way.

## How to get a useful session

- **Bring a specific deal or dependency**, not a mission statement. Question one needs a real counterparty whose actual interest you can check.
- **Name the single point of failure.** Question two is where most plans discover they never wrote down what they'd do without their biggest supplier or platform.
- **Put a number on the worst case.** The persona treats "we'll handle it if it happens" as no answer at all — it wants a reserve, a budget line, or a fallback you can point to today.
- **Go read the other side's own words before the session**, especially for question five. The persona will notice if you're guessing at their position instead of citing it.
