On November 12, 2026, [Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm) as President, CEO and a board member, effective immediately, after 33 years running the company. [Jensen Huang](/founder/ceo-advisor/jensen-huang) is already a Heropedia hero; the person who stepped into the job that same day is not, until today. Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO since 2013, agreed to serve as interim CEO while keeping her finance role. Six days later, on her first earnings call in the new job, [Nvidia posted record fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion and net income of $65.3 billion](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/11/18/nvidia-breaks-revenue-and-profit-records-and-will-keep-doing-it/5310669/), both more than double the year-earlier figures, and she closed the call by telling investors, "Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing." Heropedia's [Colette Kress persona](/founder/ceo-advisor/colette-kress) is built around the discipline that got her there: a decade of earnings calls where she insists that capital gets planned before anything else, years ahead of the demand it will serve.

This is an AI persona based on Kress's own interviews and public remarks, with every fact and quote tied to a source on the hero page. It is not Colette Kress, and neither she nor Nvidia has any affiliation with or gives any endorsement to Heropedia.

## Why her discipline is worth borrowing

Kress spent nearly three decades in finance roles at Texas Instruments, Microsoft and Cisco before Nvidia hired her as CFO in 2013, and she says the path was never a plan: "I think honestly, throughout my career, I was just always fascinated by somebody else's role." What she built instead, across years of post-earnings interviews, is a specific operating habit: plan the capital that funds the next design before the current one sells out, and state discipline in units small enough to check — "We do not waste a day, we don't waste a dime."

That habit matters more than usual right now. [A Fortune profile published the day she became interim CEO](https://fortune.com/2026/11/12/colette-kress-interim-chief-exeuctive-office-nvidia-jensen-huang-never-sought-c-suite-career-climb-billionaire-finance-leader/) notes she was never publicly positioned as Huang's successor — she is running the company on the strength of how she has run its finances, not a succession plan. The persona's questions are built from that same discipline: before you point to a strong result, it wants to know what was already funded to produce the next one.

## The six questions it will ask

Bring one real plan — a budget, a bet, a hire — because the persona pushes until your answer names a specific line item, a specific number, or a specific gap, not a general assurance that things are under control.

1. **What is funded right now to build the thing you'll need two years from now, not just what you need today?** It wants a capital commitment already in motion, with a date — her own standard is "The biggest part of planning for this size of company is planning our capital to design more products."
2. **Is there a day this week you wasted, or a dollar that bought nothing — name it?** One specific day or expense, not a claim that the team is already lean. Her own standard is unit-level: "we don't waste a dime."
3. **Your plan assumes the best outcome happens. What have you actually put in place so you're ready if it does?** A concrete readiness step tied to the optimistic case — she frames this as "assuming the best" while still asking her own team, "Do we have all of our pieces in order?"
4. **What number tells you demand is still ahead of your capacity, and what was it last quarter versus this one?** One tracked figure with both periods stated — the same discipline behind the cloud-industry backlog she cited on her first call as CEO, up from $2 trillion to $2.5 trillion in a single quarter.
5. **If this bet takes a decade rather than two years, what are you funding today that only pays off that far out?** A named investment whose payoff horizon is multiple years, because she treats the current computing shift as a commitment measured in decades: "We are not going to get this all done in two years."
6. **Walk me through whether every piece is actually in order for what you're about to do. Which piece isn't?** One honestly named gap, not a claim of full readiness.

## What it will push back on

The persona rejects treating one strong quarter as proof a plan is working on its own — Kress's own habit is to check whether capital is still ahead of demand, not behind it, before declaring success. It will also push back on plans that prepare only for the downside while an optimistic case sits with nothing built to catch it, and on vague confidence offered in place of a specific day or dollar when asked what was wasted. It will not forecast whether Nvidia names Kress permanent CEO, and it does not have a view on the leadership change itself — it works from her documented operating habits, not from speculation about what happens next.

## How to get a useful session

- **Bring one real plan**, not your whole budget. "We're funding X now to be ready for Y in 2028" works far better than "what do you think about our finances."
- **Have a number ready for question four.** The persona wants a tracked figure compared across two periods, not a sense that demand "feels" strong.
- **Expect to be asked what you wasted.** Question two is designed to surface the gap between "we're efficient" as a belief and a specific day or dollar you can actually name.
- **Decide your decade-long bet before the session**, not during it. Question five works best when you already have a candidate investment in mind to defend.
