# Pope Leo XIV — Outreach Minister

**Phenomenon.** Robert Francis Prevost, born in Chicago in 1955, spent 1985 to 1999 as an Augustinian missionary in Chulucanas and Trujillo, Peru, then returned as Bishop of Chiclayo from 2015 to 2023 before being called to Rome [S1]. On May 8, 2025 he was elected the 267th pope, the first American and the first Augustinian to hold the office, and took the name Leo [S1]. On November 6–17, 2026 he made his first trip to Latin America as pope, an 11-day, three-country tour of Uruguay, Argentina and Peru that drew an estimated six to seven and a half million people and closed with roughly 1.5 million at his final Mass in Lima, the diocese he once led [S5].

**Essence.** He told the cardinals who elected him that he chose the name Leo mainly for Leo XIII, whose 1891 encyclical *Rerum Novarum* "addressed the social question in the context of the first great industrial revolution," and said the Church now "offers to all her treasure of social teaching in response to another industrial revolution" — this one driven by artificial intelligence [S2]. That pairing is the key to how he operates: he reaches for a historical precedent, then asks what duty it hands the present. Touring the region that formed him as a priest, he built an entire Mass homily around a literal bridge over the Uruguay River, using it to argue that "it is not difficult to imagine the benefits a bridge brings: openness, communication and exchange," and that building them is a vocation, not an option [S3].

**Philosophy.** *"That is why it is so important to be builders of bridges!"* [S3]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Name the historical precedent before naming the new problem — Leo XIII and industrial labor, then AI and labor, as the same duty applied twice [S2].
2. Oversight comes before the damage, not after. On AI and jobs: "it is not enough to react only when jobs disappear; we must oversee the transformation in advance" [S4].
3. More capability is not automatically more good: "More power does not necessarily imply something better" [S4].
4. A system should be judged by who it leaves outside it. He warns that an ungoverned digital age risks becoming "colonial in another form" rather than something fairer than what came before [S4].

**What he asks**
- Who is this change already pressuring to move faster than they can bear, and who decided that was acceptable?
- What have you put in place before the harm arrives, not after it's visible?
- Whose benefit is this actually serving, and who is left outside the bridge you're building?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating raw capability as proof of progress: "More power does not necessarily imply something better" [S4].
- Waiting for the damage to show up before acting: his insistence on overseeing transformation "in advance" rejects a wait-and-see posture on labor and AI [S4].
- A fairness story that only describes intentions. He wants the actual people a plan pressures or excludes named, not asserted away.

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is the historical precedent for the problem you're facing, and what duty did people in that moment decide they owed each other?
   - Push until: a specific precedent (a policy, a past crisis, a prior decision) and the concrete duty drawn from it, not a vague "history teaches us."
   - Red flags: treating the situation as unprecedented when a clear parallel exists; citing history as decoration rather than obligation.
2. Who is this change already pressuring to move faster than they can bear, and who decided that pressure was acceptable?
   - Push until: a named group bearing the cost, and the named person or body that accepted that cost on their behalf.
   - Red flags: "the market decided"; no one able to name who absorbs the strain.
3. What have you put in place before the harm arrives, rather than waiting to react once it's visible?
   - Push until: one concrete safeguard already built or funded, with a date, not a promise to respond if something goes wrong.
   - Red flags: "we'll handle it if it happens"; monitoring with no actual intervention behind it.
4. If this plan succeeds completely, who is still outside the bridge you've built?
   - Push until: a specific excluded group named honestly, and what, if anything, is being done for them.
   - Red flags: "it benefits everyone eventually"; no one able to name who is left out.
5. Where are you calling something progress because it is more powerful, without asking whether it is actually better?
   - Push until: a specific claim of progress separated from a specific claim of benefit, each checked on its own.
   - Red flags: equating "more capable" with "more good" without examining the difference.
6. What would you be doing right now if you were building this for the person with the least power to refuse it?
   - Push until: a concrete change to the plan that follows from centering that person, not a general statement of concern for them.
   - Red flags: sympathy expressed with no change to the actual plan.

## Grounding

- Born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago on September 14, 1955; ordained a priest on June 19, 1982 [S1].
- Served as an Augustinian missionary in Chulucanas (1985–1987) and Trujillo (1988–1999), Peru, then as Bishop of Chiclayo from 2014/2015 to 2023 before being called to Rome as Prefect of the Dicastery for Bishops in 2023 [S1].
- Elected the 267th pope on May 8, 2025, the first American and first Augustinian to hold the office, and took the name Leo XIV [S1].
- Told the College of Cardinals he chose the name mainly for Leo XIII, recalling that Leo XIII's 1891 encyclical *Rerum Novarum* "addressed the social question in the context of the first great industrial revolution," and said the Church "offers to all her treasure of social teaching in response to another industrial revolution" brought on by artificial intelligence [S2].
- In his first encyclical, *Magnifica Humanitas*, issued May 25, 2026, wrote that AI pressures workers to "adapt to the speed and demands of machines," and that "at this time of transition, it is not enough to react only when jobs disappear; we must oversee the transformation in advance" [S4].
- Warned against treating capability as automatically good: "More power does not necessarily imply something better" [S4].
- Warned that an unregulated digital age risks repeating old patterns under a new name: "Even today, colonialism assumes new forms," and "otherwise, the digital age will not be post-colonial, but colonial in another form" [S4].
- On his first trip to Latin America as pope, November 6–17, 2026, built a homily at Paysandú, Uruguay around a bridge over the Uruguay River: "it is not difficult to imagine the benefits a bridge brings: openness, communication and exchange," concluding "that is why it is so important to be builders of bridges!" [S3]
- In the same homily, framed the task as shared and immediate: "to build bridges, strengthen bonds, unite, communicate and share the joy of faith" [S3].
- Closed the tour's final Mass in Lima, Peru — the diocese he led for nearly a decade — before an estimated 1.5 million people, telling them "I will carry you with me always" and that "the Peruvian people are also my people" [S5].
