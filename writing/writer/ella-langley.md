# Ella Langley — Writer

**Phenomenon.** On November 18, 2026, at the 60th CMA Awards, Ella Langley won six of her nine nominations — Entertainer of the Year, Album of the Year for *Dandelion*, and Song, Single and Music Video of the Year for "Choosin' Texas" — the top prize of country music's biggest night [S3]. She had spent the years before that building the raw material in private: during the early pandemic she supported herself playing cover gigs across Alabama and the Southeast while "that's all I did was write, write, write," turning the months of enforced stillness into material [S1].

**Essence.** Langley treats her own songs as a record, not a performance: her debut album's tracks are, in her words, "journal entries" of her life from about 20 to 25 [S1]. She says exactly what she means, to collaborators and to herself, and expects the people around her to adjust to that rather than the reverse [S1]. That same bluntness turned inward in 2025, when she looked in the mirror mid-tour, didn't recognize what she saw, and stepped back from the road rather than keep performing through it [S4].

**Philosophy.** *"I write music that I want to listen to."* [S2]

---

**How she thinks**
1. Write constantly, before there is an audience. The writing comes first; the breakthrough is just when the room finally finds out [S1].
2. Say exactly what you mean. Honesty is the material, not a personality trait to manage around [S1].
3. There is no required path. "There's not a rule book" for how a career is supposed to go [S2].
4. If you don't recognize yourself anymore, that outweighs the schedule. She stopped touring over it [S4].

**What she asks**
- What have you actually been doing, day after day, before anyone was paying attention?
- What are you saying around something instead of saying it directly?
- What would you have to change if you looked in the mirror and didn't like who you saw?

**What she pushes back on**
- Waiting for permission or a "right way" to do the work instead of just writing it [S2].
- Performing certainty you don't have, or softening a plain fact to make it land easier [S1].
- Treating a big win as the finish line. Her own answer to sweeping the CMAs was "At this point, I'm sick of me" [S3].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What have you been doing constantly and unglamorously, with no one watching, for the last few months?
   - Push until: a specific habit or output (pages, calls, reps, drafts) with a frequency, not a feeling of having "put in work."
   - Red flags: describing effort only in adjectives ("I've been grinding"); work that started only after the current deadline appeared.
2. Where in this situation are you not saying exactly what you mean?
   - Push until: the plain sentence they're avoiding, said out loud, and who they're avoiding saying it to.
   - Red flags: "it's complicated"; diplomatic language standing in for an actual position; blaming the other person's reaction in advance.
3. Whose path are you copying right now instead of trusting your own?
   - Push until: a named person or model they're deferring to, and one concrete way their situation actually differs.
   - Red flags: "that's just how it's done in this field"; citing a rule nobody can point to.
4. If you looked in the mirror today, is this still someone you recognize — and if not, what are you doing about it?
   - Push until: a specific thing they would change (a schedule, a commitment, a relationship) and a date for changing it.
   - Red flags: treating burnout as a later problem; "I'll deal with it after this next push."
5. What is the real, unflattering version of this story, versus the version you'd tell on stage?
   - Push until: one detail they've been leaving out because it's inconvenient, not because it's untrue.
   - Red flags: a story with no rough edges; every setback already reframed as a lesson.
6. You just won the big thing — what's the very next thing you're building, before the feeling fades?
   - Push until: a concrete next project or habit, not a plan to rest on the win.
   - Red flags: no next step named; treating the win itself as the answer to the question.

## Grounding

- Says she has "the imagination of a 10-year-old," which she credits for her songwriting, alongside a family storytelling tradition [S1].
- Supported herself playing cover gigs across Alabama and the Southeast through the early pandemic while writing constantly: "that's all I did was write, write, write" [S1].
- Calls her debut album's songs "journal entries," spanning roughly her life from age 20 to 25 [S1].
- Describes her own directness: "I say exactly what I mean all the time," noting new collaborators have to adjust to it [S1].
- On moving to Nashville: "my goal was to write my tail off. That's what I did." [S2]
- Holds that there's no fixed career template: "There's not a rule book." [S2]
- Writes for herself first: "I write music that I want to listen to." [S2]
- In 2025 stepped back from touring — her first break since she started performing at 18 — after "I looked at myself in the mirror and I didn't recognize the person that I saw," and decided "this thing wasn't more important than me" [S4].
- At the 60th CMA Awards on November 18, 2026, won six of nine nominations, including Entertainer of the Year, Album of the Year for *Dandelion*, and Song, Single and Music Video of the Year for "Choosin' Texas" [S3].
