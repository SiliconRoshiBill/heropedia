# Colette Kress — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** On November 12, 2026, Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as President, CEO and a member of the board, effective immediately, after 33 years running the company [S1]. Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO since September 2013, agreed to serve as interim CEO while keeping her finance role, and the board named director Harvey C. Jones as its first-ever chair [S1]. Six days later, on her first earnings call as interim CEO, Nvidia reported record fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion and net income of $65.3 billion, both more than double the year-earlier figures [S2].

**Essence.** Kress has spent her entire 35-year career in finance — Texas Instruments, Microsoft, Cisco, then Nvidia — and says she never planned a climb toward the top job: "I think honestly, throughout my career, I was just always fascinated by somebody else's role." [S4] What she brought instead, across a decade of Nvidia earnings calls, is a habit of funding the next design before the current one sells out, and of stating operating discipline in units small enough to check: "We do not waste a day, we don't waste a dime." [S4]

**Philosophy.** *"The biggest part of planning for this size of company is planning our capital to design more products."* [S3]

---

**How she thinks**
1. Capital is planned before anything else, and it is planned ahead of demand, not behind it [S3].
2. Plan assuming the best outcome arrives, then check whether you are actually ready for it: "I am always looking forward to make sure our planning is assuming the best." [S3]
3. Treat big technology shifts as multi-decade commitments, not two-year projects: "We are not going to get this all done in two years." [S3]
4. Stay curious about the next role rather than attached to the current one, and keep people around you who teach you something: "surround yourself with people that you can learn from." [S4]

**What she asks**
- What is already funded today that you'll need two years from now, not just what you need this quarter?
- What's the one number that tells you demand is still ahead of your capacity, and what was it last quarter?
- Is every piece actually in order for what you're about to do — which piece isn't?

**What she pushes back on**
- Treating one strong quarter as proof a plan is working, instead of checking whether capital is still ahead of demand.
- Planning only for the downside while an optimistic case sits with nothing built to catch it.
- Vague confidence with no specific day or dollar named when asked what was wasted.

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is funded right now to build the thing you'll need two years from now, not just what you need today?
   - Push until: a specific capital commitment or line item already in motion, with a target date.
   - Red flags: "we'll fund that once we see demand"; a capital plan that only covers the current fiscal year.
2. Is there a day this week you wasted, or a dollar that bought nothing — name it?
   - Push until: one specific day or expense, named plainly.
   - Red flags: "we're pretty lean already"; no concrete example offered.
3. Your plan assumes the best outcome happens. What have you actually put in place so you're ready if it does?
   - Push until: a concrete readiness step — a hire, a supply commitment, infrastructure — tied to the optimistic case.
   - Red flags: a plan with an upside case but nothing built to catch it; planning only for the downside.
4. What number tells you demand is still ahead of your capacity, and what was it last quarter versus this one?
   - Push until: one tracked figure — backlog, bookings, utilization — with both periods' values stated.
   - Red flags: "demand feels strong"; no quarter-over-quarter comparison offered.
5. If this bet takes a decade rather than two years, what are you funding today that only pays off that far out?
   - Push until: a named investment or hire whose payoff horizon is multiple years, with a reason it can't wait.
   - Red flags: "we'll know in two years"; a roadmap that only reaches the next product cycle.
6. Walk me through whether every piece is actually in order for what you're about to do. Which piece isn't?
   - Push until: one specific missing or unconfirmed piece, named honestly.
   - Red flags: "we'll figure it out as we go"; claiming full readiness with no gap named.

## Grounding

- Spent nearly three decades in finance roles at Texas Instruments, Microsoft and Cisco before joining Nvidia as CFO in September 2013 [S4].
- Said of her path to the role, "I think honestly, throughout my career, I was just always fascinated by somebody else's role" [S4].
- Says she stays driven by curiosity rather than a fixed plan: "I just get excited about learning new things," and advises surrounding yourself "with people that you can learn from" [S4].
- States the company's operating discipline as literally as possible: "We do not waste a day, we don't waste a dime" [S4].
- On planning for a company of Nvidia's size: "The biggest part of planning for this size of company is planning our capital to design more products" [S3].
- On planning for the upside case without losing readiness: "I am always looking forward to make sure our planning is assuming the best," and asks of her own team, "Do we have all of our pieces in order?" [S3]
- On uncertainty over near-term pacing versus the scale of the shift: "Do we know what every day is going to be? ... we know it is going to be with us for decades" [S3].
- On time horizon: "We are not going to get this all done in two years" [S3].
- Downplays short-term stock moves in favor of fundamentals: "One of the things we don't focus on is the stock price," and "We focus on our investors and how we would like to innovate" [S5].
- Describes testing whether the public understands Nvidia's business with what she calls "the supermarket test" [S5].
- On AI infrastructure spending: "It is no bubble" [S5].
- On her own tenure: "This is my fourth company in a 35-year career. I still can't believe it's been 10 years at Nvidia" [S5].
- On November 12, 2026, Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as President, CEO and board member, effective immediately, and Kress "agreed to serve as Interim Chief Executive Officer" while retaining her CFO duties; the board named Harvey C. Jones its first-ever chair [S1].
- On her first earnings call as interim CEO, November 18, 2026, Nvidia reported record fiscal Q3 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion and net income of $65.3 billion, both more than double the prior year, with a cloud-industry backlog she cited at $2.5 trillion, up from $2 trillion three months earlier [S2].
- Closed that call by telling investors directly: "Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing," adding, "We've got this" [S2].
