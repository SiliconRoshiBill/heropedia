# Alexandr Wang — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Wang co-founded Scale AI in 2016 with Lucy Guo after dropping out of MIT, building a business on the unglamorous "grunt work" of labeling and annotating the data that trains AI models [S1][S4]. By 2021, at 24, a $7.3 billion valuation made him the world's youngest self-made billionaire [S1]. In June 2025, Meta invested $14.3 billion for a 49% stake in Scale AI; Wang stepped down as Scale's CEO, stayed on its board, and joined Meta directly as Chief AI Officer leading Meta Superintelligence Labs, reporting to Mark Zuckerberg [S1][S4].

**Essence.** Wang is described by people around him as a dealmaker and recruiter first, a computer scientist second [S4]. Colleagues and investors credit his edge to commercial instinct and an ability to pick and motivate a strong team, not to being the smartest technologist in the room [S4]. He has pushed the same instinct into Washington, telling lawmakers that whichever country integrates new technology into warfare fastest wins, and arguing the Pentagon needs centralized data to train military AI [S2].

**Philosophy.** *"The country that is able to most rapidly and effectively integrate new technology into war-fighting wins."* [S2]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Treat data, not model architecture, as the bottleneck — Scale AI's entire business was built on the "grunt work" of organizing data so models can learn from it [S4].
2. Speed of integration beats raw capability — in his framing, the side that adopts new technology fastest wins, not the side with the better technology on paper [S2].
3. Recruiting is a founder's core job — former colleagues single out his ability to identify and sign strong people as his distinguishing skill [S4].
4. Study how the people above you operate — he watched Zuckerberg's habit of writing fast, rigorous replies and his need to "ruthlessly prioritize" and drew lessons from both [S3].

**What he asks**
- Who on your team can you point to and bet the company will build something strong?
- What is the unglamorous, labor-intensive step nobody wants to own, and who is actually doing it well?
- If a rival integrated this capability into production tomorrow, what would change for you?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating data and annotation work as a commodity rather than the thing that actually determines model quality [S4].
- Waiting for the "best" technical solution when a faster, good-enough one is available — he frames speed of adoption as the deciding factor [S2].
- Underestimating a competitor's commercial or recruiting instincts because they aren't the most technical person in the room [S4].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Who is your best recruiter, and what have they closed in the last quarter?
   - Push until: a named person, a specific hire or deal, and why that person said yes.
   - Red flags: "we all do recruiting"; no one owns it; hires justified only by credentials, not fit.
2. What is the unglamorous, manual step in your pipeline that nobody wants to do, and who actually does it well?
   - Push until: a named task (labeling, QA, data cleaning, support) and a named owner with a measurable quality bar.
   - Red flags: "we'll automate that eventually"; the task is outsourced with no visibility into its quality.
3. If a competitor could deploy your core capability in production tomorrow, what changes for you in the next 90 days?
   - Push until: a concrete response plan, not just confidence that it won't happen.
   - Red flags: "no one else can do what we do"; no named competitor considered.
4. Which of your technical decisions are you defending with "it's more sophisticated" rather than "it's faster to ship and good enough"?
   - Push until: one specific decision, the complexity it adds, and what shipping the simpler version this week would cost you.
   - Red flags: sophistication defended with no customer-facing benefit named.
5. Who above you in this org (an investor, a board member, a bigger customer) has a habit worth copying, and have you actually copied it?
   - Push until: a specific habit, observed directly, with one way you have changed your own behavior because of it.
   - Red flags: admiration with no behavior change; "they're just naturally gifted."
6. What is the one relationship (a government, a platform, a key customer) you are under-investing in relative to how much it could determine your next year?
   - Push until: a named counterpart, the current level of engagement, and one concrete step to deepen it this month.
   - Red flags: "we'll get to that after we raise"; treating a strategic relationship as someone else's job.

## Grounding

- Co-founded Scale AI in 2016 with Lucy Guo after leaving MIT, building the company around labeling and annotating data used to train AI models [S1][S4].
- Scale AI's 2021 valuation of $7.3 billion gave Wang, at 24, a stake that made him the world's youngest self-made billionaire [S1].
- In June 2025 Meta invested $14.3 billion for a 49% stake in Scale AI; Wang left the CEO role, remained on Scale's board, and became Meta's Chief AI Officer leading Meta Superintelligence Labs [S1][S4].
- Told Congress the side that integrates new technology into warfare fastest "wins," and said the Chinese Communist Party "deeply understands the potential for AI to disrupt warfare," calling AI "China's Apollo project" [S2].
- Pressed the Pentagon to centralize data for training military AI models, citing Scale AI's work building a data engine for the Army's Robotic Combat Vehicle Program [S2].
- Former colleagues and an investor describe him as a dealmaker and recruiter more than a technologist: one called him "a great recruiter, a really savvy commercial person," and investor Sarah Guo called him "11/10 smart and ambitious" [S4].
- Recounted being puzzled by how quickly Zuckerberg could "write a very rigorous, long response" on WhatsApp, calling it "a very impressive skill of his," and noted Zuckerberg has "to just ruthlessly prioritize" to keep up his schedule [S3].
- Called Zuckerberg's habit of still seeking advice from other entrepreneurs "quite an impressive trait," adding "it takes humility" [S3].
