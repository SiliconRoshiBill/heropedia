# David Sacks — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Sacks joined Confinity, soon renamed PayPal, in 1999 as its first product leader and later COO, through its 2002 IPO and $1.5 billion sale to eBay [S5]. He founded the genealogy site Geni.com in 2006, and in 2008 spun an internal Geni communication tool into Yammer with co-founder Adam Pisoni; Microsoft bought Yammer, where he was CEO, for $1.2 billion in 2012 [S1][S5]. After investing in Zenefits in 2014, he stepped in as its interim CEO in January 2016 during a regulatory crisis, serving about ten months [S5]. He co-founded Craft Ventures in late 2017 with an initial $350 million fund, and co-hosts the All-In podcast [S1][S5]. Trump named him White House AI and crypto czar on December 5, 2024; he stepped down on March 26, 2026, citing the 130-day-per-year cap on special government employees, while staying on as co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST) with Michael Kratsios [S4][S5].

**Essence.** Sacks runs every pitch and every board meeting through the same small set of ratios: burn as a multiple of net-new ARR, CAC split by channel rather than blended, and dollar expansion from surviving accounts measured against dollar churn [S3]. He wants to see the product working before he hears the narrative around it [S1]. He treats founder intensity as necessary but double-edged — useful until it stops a founder from taking advice [S3] — and treats disorder in a growing company as the expected cost of growth, not a sign something is broken [S2].

**Philosophy.** *"If you're burning more than three times your net new ARR, I would define that as being suspect or bad."* [S3]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Compute burn as a multiple of net-new ARR, not as a standalone dollar figure — above roughly 3x is a warning sign [S3].
2. Split CAC by channel instead of blending it, because a single blended number hides which channel is actually working [S3].
3. Judge retention by a dollar race: does expansion revenue from accounts that stayed beat the dollars lost to churn [S3]?
4. See the product before the pitch — open a new-startup meeting with a live demo [S1].
5. Expect growth itself to generate chaos, and build a simple process to diagnose and correct it rather than treating it as a crisis [S2].

**What he asks**
- What's your CAC broken out by channel, not blended into one number?
- Is the dollar expansion from accounts that stayed with you bigger than what you lost to churn this quarter?
- What's your burn this quarter as a multiple of your net-new ARR?

**What he pushes back on**
- A single blended CAC presented as if it describes the whole business [S3].
- Logo churn treated as a crisis on its own, without checking whether expansion dollars from retained accounts outweigh it [S3].
- Founder conviction that has tipped into refusing outside advice [S3].
- A narrative pitch offered in place of a working product demo [S1].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is your CAC, broken out by channel?
   - Push until: an actual dollar CAC for at least two or three distinct channels, not one blended figure.
   - Red flags: "our CAC is $X" with no channel breakdown; all acquisition spend lumped together.
2. What is your burn multiple this quarter — burn divided by net-new ARR?
   - Push until: a computed ratio with the underlying burn and net-new ARR numbers stated.
   - Red flags: a multiple above 3 excused as "we're investing in growth"; no net-new ARR figure offered at all.
3. Does the dollar expansion from accounts that stayed with you exceed what you lost to churn this quarter?
   - Push until: two dollar figures side by side — expansion revenue and churned revenue — and which one is larger.
   - Red flags: citing a low logo-churn percentage while never naming the dollar figures on either side.
4. Who is actually on your board, and what did you bring each of them on to do?
   - Push until: each member named with the one specific strength or job they're there for.
   - Red flags: a board assembled for prestige or signaling, with no one able to push back on the CEO.
5. Where does your own conviction stop being useful and start blocking you from advice?
   - Push until: a specific recent decision where someone else's input changed your mind, or an honest admission that none has.
   - Red flags: "I trust my gut" offered as a complete answer, with no example of being corrected.
6. Can you show me the product working right now, before we discuss anything else?
   - Push until: an actual live demo or screen-share of the real product, not slides describing it.
   - Red flags: "it's not ready to show"; a roadmap deck substituted for a working demo.

- Stepped down as AI and crypto czar on March 26, 2026, citing the 130-day-per-year limit on special government employees, and said of his continuing, broader PCAST role, "I can now make recommendations on not just about AI" and "this is how I will be involved moving forward" [S4].
