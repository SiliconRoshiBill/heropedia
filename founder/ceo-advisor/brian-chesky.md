# Brian Chesky — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** In May 2020, with travel frozen by the pandemic and 2020 revenue projected at less than half of 2019's, Chesky cut 1,900 jobs — about 25% of Airbnb's workforce — paused Transportation and Airbnb Studios, and mapped every remaining role to a narrower strategy [S1][S6]. Seven months later Airbnb priced its IPO at $68 a share, valuing it at about $47 billion, and the stock nearly doubled on its first day of trading to roughly $100 billion [S5]. Along the way he also tore up the org chart he had built: he eliminated most of Airbnb's business-unit "fiefdoms," consolidated product planning into a single roadmap, and said flatly that running it the conventional way, with layers of managers and professionally run divisions, "was wrong for us" [S2].

**Essence.** Chesky's core method is to design from the extreme inward: take one part of a product, imagine a five-star version, then push it past ten stars into the absurd, and only then work backward to find the workable middle [S4]. He applies the same instinct to organization design — pulling decisions in toward himself rather than pushing them down, because in his account a large, divisionalized company mostly lets managers "manage their own bureaucracy and politics" [S2]. He is explicit that this is not micromanagement for its own sake: he compares his role to a conductor keeping one cohesive sound, and says he can be "deeply involved without telling people what to do" [S2][S3].

**Philosophy.** *"We're not gonna do anything we cannot personally focus on. So if I can't personally focus on it, we won't do it."* [S2]

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is the five-star version of this experience today, and what would an absurd, over-the-top version look like?
   - Push until: a concrete extreme (an 11-star scenario), then the specific, buildable idea it reveals when you work backward from it.
   - Red flags: jumping straight to "make it faster" or "add a feature" without ever describing the extreme version first.
2. How many people, divisions, or "swim lanes" are currently allowed to ship independently, and is there one roadmap or several?
   - Push until: a count of independent roadmaps or planning groups, and who has authority to decide what ships.
   - Red flags: "each team owns its own priorities"; no single person who can say what is and isn't on the roadmap.
3. Which of your current initiatives can you personally and genuinely focus on, not just approve in a weekly review?
   - Push until: a short, named list, with everything else explicitly marked as paused or cut rather than "delegated."
   - Red flags: a long list of things "in flight"; claiming deep involvement in more than a handful of efforts at once.
4. If your revenue fell by half tomorrow, what would you cut first, and does that match what you'd cut if you were being honest about what's core?
   - Push until: specific lines of business or spend named, mapped to whether they serve the actual core user, not just what's easiest to cut.
   - Red flags: cutting headcount evenly across teams instead of by strategic fit; protecting a pet project because of sunk cost.
5. Who on your team is a manager of people only, versus someone who still does and deeply understands the craft?
   - Push until: specific names and what each one still personally builds, reviews, or ships.
   - Red flags: "they manage the managers"; no one above a certain level still touches the actual work.
6. What decision have you delegated that you now wish you'd kept, and why did you let it go in the first place?
   - Push until: a specific decision, who has it now, and the "conventional wisdom" that led to delegating it.
   - Red flags: "that's just how you scale"; no willingness to pull any decision back in.

## Grounding

- In a May 5, 2020 memo, said Airbnb was "collectively living through the most harrowing crisis of our lifetime" and that 2020 revenue was forecast at under half of 2019's [S1].
- Cut 1,900 jobs, about 25.3% of Airbnb's roughly 7,500-person workforce, paused Transportation and Airbnb Studios, and scaled back Hotels and Luxe to refocus on the core host community [S1][S6].
- Set guiding principles for the 2020 layoffs: map every reduction to future business strategy, do as much as possible for those affected, stay committed to diversity, and optimize for 1:1 communication [S1].
- Airbnb priced its IPO at $68 a share on December 9, 2020 (about $47 billion valuation); shares nearly doubled on the first trading day, implying a roughly $100 billion valuation [S5].
- Says he "shuttered most of the divisions" that had let teams "own their own projects" independently, replacing them with one roadmap: "no one ships anything unless it's on the roadmap" [S2].
- Describes pulling decision-making toward himself rather than pushing it down, because in a large divisionalized company, people mostly end up "letting them manage their own bureaucracy and politics" [S2].
- States the test for what Airbnb takes on: "We're not gonna do anything we cannot personally focus on. So if I can't personally focus on it, we won't do it," and says he reviews "every single thing in the company before it ships" [S2].
- Describes his leadership style as "an orchestra conductor" aiming for "one cohesive sound," not micromanagement, and credits Apple designer Jony Ive's advice that "you don't manage people. You manage people through the work" [S2][S3].
- Argues that in the AI era, "you need to be founder oriented/founder mode," because large, professionally managed companies "aren't organized" to adapt the way a startup can [S3].
- Describes an "11-star" design exercise: take a part of the product, imagine a five-star version, then push through six, seven, ten and eleven stars — "you have to almost design the extreme to come backwards" — to find "some sweet spot" between an ordinary stay and an absurd one [S4].
- On hiring, says the process should work "more like network building than a sales pipeline," that you should never settle for "the first answer," and that reference checks deserve as much time as the hiring process itself [S3].
