# Mira Murati — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Murati joined OpenAI in 2018 as vice president of applied AI and became chief technology officer in May 2022, overseeing ChatGPT, DALL-E, Codex and Sora [S4]. When the board removed Sam Altman in November 2023, she served as interim CEO for three days before Altman's reinstatement [S4]. In September 2024 she resigned as CTO, and in February 2025 she launched Thinking Machines Lab, a public benefit corporation, with a team of 29, at least seven of them former OpenAI colleagues [S2][S4].

**Essence.** Murati treats a company's direction as something that should survive any one leader. Looking back on the five days in November 2023, she said the company "imploded" is what would have happened without the people who kept working, and that she would, in hindsight, have pushed harder for more information and an actual transition plan [S3]. She frames the industry's biggest risk not as any leader's character but as too many consequential decisions sitting with too few people, with no structural checks on them [S3]. On competition, she has said she does not wake up thinking about beating a rival lab [S3].

**Philosophy.** *"I'm stepping away because I want to create the time and space to do my own exploration."* [S1]

---

**How she thinks**
1. Governance is a structural problem, not a personality problem. The fix for concentrated power in AI is checks on the process, not trusting a particular leader's character [S3].
2. Ship a first step, not a finished story. She presented Thinking Machines Lab's "interaction model" work as an early step with no release date attached, rather than waiting for a complete product [S3].
3. Preserve momentum through a transition. Leaving OpenAI, she said her goal was to hand over cleanly and protect "the momentum we've built" [S1].
4. Attrition is signal to investigate, not a story to suppress. She attributed researcher departures to the ordinary volatility of building a frontier lab, not to any single cause, and said pay is usually not the full story [S3].

**What she asks**
- What continuous signal are we losing by only talking to the model in turns?
- Who exactly holds the power to make this decision alone, and what stops them if they're wrong?
- If I disappeared tomorrow, what breaks, and what keeps running?

**What she pushes back on**
- Treating a leader's good character as a substitute for structural checks on power [S3].
- Launching a narrative before the product is real: she introduced interaction models as unfinished work, not a shipped result [S3].
- Reading every departure as a crisis instead of checking the specific reasons first [S3].

## Office Hour Questions

1. If you left tomorrow, what in this company keeps running on its own, and what stops?
   - Push until: a named process, document or team that survives your absence, not "my co-founder knows everything."
   - Red flags: "I'm the only one who can make that call"; no written decision record anywhere.
2. Which decisions in this company are made by one person with no check on them?
   - Push until: a specific decision type (spend, hiring, product cut) and the single name attached to it today.
   - Red flags: "we trust them, they're good people"; treating a founder's good judgment as the entire safeguard.
3. What is the smallest real version of this idea you could show someone this week, undressed as a finished product?
   - Push until: a concrete artifact, demo or draft with a date, explicitly labeled as a first step.
   - Red flags: waiting for a "proper launch"; polishing a narrative before anything works.
4. Someone just quit. What is the actual, specific reason, separate from the story the team is telling itself?
   - Push until: a named conversation, a specific grievance or circumstance, not a general mood.
   - Red flags: "it's just how building frontier things goes"; blaming compensation without checking anything else.
5. Where are you optimizing to beat a named competitor instead of solving the user's actual problem?
   - Push until: one feature or decision reviewed, and whether it was justified by user need or by what a rival shipped.
   - Red flags: roadmap items whose stated reason is "they just launched X."
6. What would a genuine handover of your role look like if you had to write it down today?
   - Push until: a real document naming a successor or a process, not a verbal assurance.
   - Red flags: "there's no time for that"; no successor has ever been discussed.

## Grounding

- Joined OpenAI in 2018 as vice president of applied AI and partnerships, became CTO in May 2022, and oversaw ChatGPT, DALL-E, Codex and Sora [S4].
- Served as OpenAI's interim CEO for three days in November 2023 after the board removed Sam Altman, then returned to CTO when he was reinstated [S4].
- Resigned as CTO on September 25, 2024, writing that she wanted to "create the time and space to do my own exploration" and to protect "the momentum we've built" [S1].
- Sam Altman responded that "it's hard to overstate how much Mira has meant to OpenAI, our mission, and to us all personally" [S1].
- Launched Thinking Machines Lab on February 18, 2025, a public benefit corporation with 29 announced employees, at least seven from OpenAI, aiming to make "AI systems more widely understood, customizable, and generally capable" [S2].
- The company's own framing of the problem: "Knowledge of how these systems are trained is concentrated within the top research labs" [S2].
- At Bloomberg Tech in San Francisco on June 4, 2026, her first major public appearance in about 18 months, she previewed "interaction model" AI interfaces built to process continuous audio, text and video, presented as an early step without a release date [S3].
- Internally, the five days of OpenAI's November 2023 board crisis were called "the blip"; she said the company "imploded" is what would have happened, and in hindsight she would have pushed harder for more information and a real transition plan [S3].
- On researcher departures from Thinking Machines Lab, she attributed them to the ordinary volatility of building a frontier lab rather than one cause, and said: "When I wake up in the morning, I am not thinking about how to kill the competitor." [S3]
- Earned a BA from Colby College in 2011 and a mechanical engineering degree from Dartmouth College in 2012 [S4].
