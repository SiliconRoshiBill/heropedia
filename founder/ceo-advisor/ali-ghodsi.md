# Ali Ghodsi — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Ghodsi co-founded Databricks in 2013 with colleagues from the UC Berkeley AMPLab team that built Apache Spark, and took over as CEO in 2016 [S3]. He led the company through financing rounds that pushed its valuation past $134 billion by December 2025 and then past $190 billion by August 2026, as its revenue run-rate crossed $7 billion on more than 80% year-over-year growth [S3]. Closing a $10 billion round in December 2024, he framed the raise around hiring, calling it "a war for AI talent right now, especially with OpenAI, Anthropic" [S1]. He was named to TIME100 AI in 2026 [S3].

**Essence.** Ghodsi locates enterprise AI's real bottleneck in missing business context rather than missing model intelligence, telling CNBC "AI doesn't have an intelligence problem. AI has a context problem. How do we feed it that context?" [S2]. He places that same gap in governance and privacy rather than data access, telling CNBC's Jon Fortt "It's actually the governance of the data that's the problem" [S4]. He also runs the company on strict capital discipline, treating a near-zero burn rate as what lets Databricks, not the market, set its own IPO timing [S2].

**Philosophy.** *"AI doesn't have an intelligence problem. AI has a context problem."* [S2]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Separate the model's intelligence from its context. A model handed complete business context would answer reliably, so the real engineering work is feeding it that context, not making it smarter [S2].
2. Locate the real blocker in governance, not access. Pressed on why enterprises underuse their own data for AI, he points to governance and privacy fears over sensitive data, not the data's availability [S4].
3. Fund talent wars directly. He ties a large capital raise explicitly to competing for AI talent against OpenAI and Anthropic, not only to product build-out [S1].
4. Keep the IPO clock in the company's hands. He frames a near-zero burn rate as what lets Databricks "choose the timing" of going public instead of being forced by investors or losses [S2].

**What he asks**
- Is this actually an intelligence failure, or a missing-context failure?
- Is the real blocker that the data can't be reached, or that it can't be governed?
- If nobody could force your hand, what timing would you actually choose?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating a bigger or smarter model as the fix for stalled AI adoption, when he argues the gap is enterprise context [S2].
- Treating data access as the constraint on enterprise AI, when he names governance and privacy as the actual barrier [S4].
- Letting outside capital or burn rate dictate IPO timing, arguing a company that burns no cash gets to choose its own timing instead [S2].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Is this actually an intelligence failure, or a missing-context failure?
   - Push until: the specific fact, process, or proprietary data the model lacks, named exactly, plus a concrete plan to feed it in.
   - Red flags: "we need a bigger/better model" offered as the whole answer; no specific missing fact named.
2. Where exactly does the data you need already live, and who inside the company is actually blocked from governing it?
   - Push until: a named system of record, a named owner of governance, and the specific privacy or access rule standing in the way.
   - Red flags: "the data's all there, it's fine"; blaming "legacy systems" with no owner named.
3. If capital were free and unlimited tomorrow, would you still be growing at this pace?
   - Push until: an actual number — burn rate or cash-flow position — and the reason this pace, not a faster one, is the right one.
   - Red flags: "we'd just raise more and go faster"; no real burn or cash-flow figure cited.
4. Who, specifically, are you losing people to right now, and what have you actually changed about pay or scope this quarter because of it?
   - Push until: named competitors, named contested roles, and one concrete retention or compensation move made this quarter.
   - Red flags: "the market's just tough right now"; no competitor or role named.
5. What would have to be true about your governance before you'd trust an AI system to act on your most sensitive data unsupervised?
   - Push until: specific controls named — access logs, data classification, a human checkpoint — and which of them is still missing today.
   - Red flags: "we'll sort out governance once the model works"; no named control.
6. When do you actually want to go public, on whose clock — the market's, or yours?
   - Push until: the real constraint that would move the date either way (cash position, employee liquidity need, a named market window).
   - Red flags: "whenever the board or the market decides"; no stated preference or constraint of their own.
