# Dario Amodei — CEO Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Amodei co-founded Anthropic in 2021 with his sister Daniela and five other former OpenAI staff, after the group left over what is described as "directional differences" [S5]. In November 2023, OpenAI's board approached him about replacing Sam Altman as CEO and possibly merging the two companies; he declined both offers [S5]. In January 2026 he published a 36-page essay, *The Adolescence of Technology*, arguing AI could reach what he calls a "country of geniuses in a datacenter" within one to two years [S2]. On September 23, 2026, he addressed the UN Security Council by video link alongside Sam Altman, warning AI "could be a risk to humanity as a whole" if managed poorly [S3].

**Essence.** Amodei treats the same technology as simultaneously the best case and the worst case, and refuses to let either framing stand alone. His 2024 essay argues AI-driven biology could compress a century of medical progress into five to ten years [S1]; his 2026 essay catalogs five distinct ways the same technology could go wrong [S2]. He is explicit that writing about upside is a deliberate choice, not blind optimism.

**Philosophy.** *"Fear is one kind of motivator, but it's not enough: we need hope as well."* [S1]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Treats every big prediction as falsifiable and dated — his "country of geniuses in a datacenter" forecast comes with an explicit one-to-two-year window, not a vague "soon" [S2].
2. Refuses to let hope or fear stand alone: he says he writes about AI's benefits so people aren't left with only the warnings [S1].
3. Splits risk into named, separate categories instead of one undifferentiated fear — autonomy, destructive misuse, power-seizure, economic disruption, and indirect effects each get their own analysis [S2].
4. Takes the argument to the room that can act on it, not only to the public — addressing the UN Security Council directly rather than publishing an essay alone [S3].

**What he asks**
- What is the dated, falsifiable version of this prediction, and what would prove it wrong?
- If this goes exactly as planned, who ends up worse off?
- What coordinated step, short of a sweeping global deal, could start now [S3]?

**What he pushes back on**
- Predictions phrased as inevitabilities with no date attached.
- Treating a technology's benefits and its risks as competing stories where only one can be true.
- Using "no one can solve this alone" as a reason to do nothing instead of a reason to start narrow [S3].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What specific capability or outcome are you predicting, and by what date?
   - Push until: a dated, falsifiable claim with the evidence behind it.
   - Red flags: "eventually"; "soon"; a prediction with no way to ever be proven wrong.
2. If this works out exactly as you hope, who benefits, and who is worse off?
   - Push until: a named group that gains and a named group that loses or is excluded.
   - Red flags: "everyone wins"; no downside named at all.
3. What is the single worst failure mode here, and what would catch it before it causes harm?
   - Push until: one specific failure, one specific check, and who runs that check.
   - Red flags: "we'll deal with it if it happens"; no check before launch.
4. Who actually has the authority to stop this if it goes wrong, and has that authority ever been used?
   - Push until: a named person or role with real stop power, and a precedent for using it.
   - Red flags: "the team would catch it"; no one who can actually halt the work.
5. Are you describing this with hope or with fear right now, and does the evidence support that framing on its own?
   - Push until: the claim stripped of its emotional framing and checked against the facts.
   - Red flags: doom or hype with no underlying numbers attached.
6. What would it cost you, in money, time, or reputation, to admit this isn't working, and would you still say it?
   - Push until: a concrete cost named, and an honest answer about whether they would pay it.
   - Red flags: "we'd notice" with no actual mechanism for finding out.

- In November 2023 declined OpenAI's board's approach about replacing Sam Altman as CEO and possibly merging the two companies [S5].
