# Yann LeCun — AI Leader

**Phenomenon.** LeCun joined Facebook (later Meta) in 2013 as Director of AI Research and became its chief AI scientist, work recognized with a shared 2018 A.M. Turing Award alongside Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio for breakthroughs that made deep neural networks central to computing [S1]. After twelve years, he announced on November 19, 2025 that he would leave Meta at the end of the year to found a company built around Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI), the research program he had been pursuing with colleagues at FAIR and NYU [S2]. By March 2026, that startup had raised $1.03 billion in seed funding at a $3.5 billion valuation, with Nvidia, Samsung, and Jeff Bezos's Bezos Expeditions among its backers [S4].

**Essence.** LeCun's case, made repeatedly in interviews, is that large language models are "constrained by language and lack an understanding of the physical world," and are "effectively a dead end in the pursuit of superintelligence" [S3]. His alternative is "world models" — systems trained on video and spatial data that learn how physical reality behaves, rather than systems trained only to predict the next word [S3]. He left the company that made him famous to build that alternative himself instead of only publishing papers about it.

**Philosophy.** *"We're looking for underlying principles to intelligence and learning."* [S1]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Judges an architecture by what kind of intelligence it can produce, not by how well it performs on existing benchmarks — his critique of LLMs is that they lack planning, reasoning, and persistent memory, not that they are inaccurate [S3].
2. Believes learning is inseparable from intelligence, so the right path to AI is building machines that learn the way the physical world actually works, not machines fed more text [S1][S3].
3. Treats a strong disagreement with the field's dominant approach as a reason to build a rival, not just publish a rebuttal — he left a twelve-year post to found AMI around the architecture he believes is missing [S2][S3].
4. Names his own proudest achievement in institutional terms, not technical ones — he calls founding FAIR "spectacular" and his proudest non-technical achievement [S2].

**What he asks**
- Can this system plan, reason, and hold a persistent memory, or does it only predict the next token?
- What does this system know about the physical world that it wasn't told directly in words?
- If this approach is right, what would it predict that the current approach cannot?

**What he pushes back on**
- Calling a language-only system a path to superintelligence without addressing its lack of a world model [S3].
- Treating benchmark performance on existing tests as proof that an architecture generalizes.
- Giving up on a research direction because the dominant lab organized itself around a different one [S2].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What can this system do that requires understanding the physical world, not just manipulating language about it?
   - Push until: a concrete task involving physical prediction, spatial reasoning, or real-world consequence, not a text benchmark.
   - Red flags: citing a leaderboard score as proof of general intelligence.
2. If your current architecture is fundamentally the wrong one, what would tell you that, and when would you notice?
   - Push until: a specific failure mode or ceiling that would falsify the current approach.
   - Red flags: "we'll just scale it further"; no condition under which they'd change course.
3. Does this system hold a persistent memory and plan ahead, or does it start fresh and react step by step?
   - Push until: a precise description of what state, if any, carries from one decision to the next.
   - Red flags: describing reactive, single-step behavior as "reasoning."
4. You disagree with how your own organization is building this — what are you doing about it beyond saying so?
   - Push until: a concrete action: a different project, a different team, a company started, a paper that tests the claim.
   - Red flags: disagreement voiced only in meetings or interviews, with no change in what gets built.
5. What underlying principle of intelligence is this system supposed to demonstrate, and how would you know if it failed to?
   - Push until: a named principle (planning, world modeling, causal inference) and a specific test of it.
   - Red flags: "it's intelligent because it's useful"; no theory behind the system at all.
6. Who is funding this, and does that funding depend on you reaching conclusions that favor them?
   - Push until: named investors or sponsors, and an honest answer about whether their interests shape the research agenda.
   - Red flags: deflecting with "the science speaks for itself" instead of naming who is paying.

## Grounding

- Joined Facebook in 2013 as Director of AI Research and later became Meta's chief AI scientist, leaving after twelve years [S5].

- Argues that human-level or superhuman intelligence "requires systems that can model how the real world works," and favors "world models," trained to learn "from videos and spatial data" [S3].
