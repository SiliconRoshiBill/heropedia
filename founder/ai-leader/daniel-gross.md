# Daniel Gross — AI Leader

**Phenomenon.** Gross co-founded Greplin, later renamed Cue, which Apple acquired in October 2013 for a reported $40–60 million before shutting the product down [S1]. He joined Y Combinator as a partner in 2017 and, with Nat Friedman, built AI Grant, giving no-strings grants to people working on interesting AI problems regardless of credentials [S2]. The two later identified compute, not capital, as the actual bottleneck facing AI startups and built the roughly $100 million Andromeda Cluster to give portfolio companies direct GPU access [S3]. In June 2024 he co-founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. with Ilya Sutskever and Daniel Levy and served as its CEO, then left on June 29, 2025 to join Meta's new Superintelligence Labs as Sutskever took over as SSI's CEO [S4]. As of January 2026 he co-leads Meta's "Meta Compute" initiative alongside Meta's head of global infrastructure Santosh Janardhan [S5].

**Essence.** Gross keeps redirecting attention from the resource everyone is fighting over to the one that is actually scarce: he decided funding wasn't the constraint on AI startups and built infrastructure around compute instead [S3]. He evaluates people the same way — not by credential, but by a specific signal he watches for in the room, and he changes what he weighs as a person moves from junior to senior [S6].

**Philosophy.** *"I think a great emotion to be on the lookout for in an interview, in particular when assessing founders, is fear."* [S6]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Find the resource that is actually binding, not the one everyone complains about. AI Grant and Andromeda both followed from deciding compute, not funding, was the real bottleneck for AI startups [S3].
2. Screen for emotion before résumé. He treats fear in a founder interview as "a very promising sign," not a disqualifier [S6].
3. Change the test as the person changes stage. Intelligence matters, "but you want to switch to caring about just how vigorous and how energetic that person is" once someone is more senior [S6].
4. Fund the person before the credential exists to validate them — AI Grant was built to find non-traditional machine learning engineers who lacked things like a Stanford PhD [S2].

**What he asks**
- What is the actual bottleneck here — capital, compute, or people — and how do you know it isn't something else?
- When you were last excited about a candidate, what did you notice them feel, not just what did they say?
- Are you still testing for the same thing you tested for when this person, or this company, was new?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating funding as the constraint when the real limit is something else, like compute access [S3].
- Using credentials as a proxy for capability instead of building a way to find people credentials miss [S2].
- Applying one fixed hiring bar at every stage instead of shifting from raw horsepower toward energy and vigor as people grow senior [S6].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is the actual bottleneck in your business right now, and how do you know it isn't a different one than the one you're complaining about?
   - Push until: a specific resource they tried to get and struggled with, with a number — months waited, dollars spent, people turned away.
   - Red flags: "we need more funding" with no attempt to test whether something else is actually binding.
2. When you last interviewed someone you ended up excited about, what did you notice them feel, not just what they said?
   - Push until: a specific emotional read — fear, hunger, discomfort — tied to a concrete moment in that interview.
   - Red flags: listing school, prior company or title as the entire case for the hire.
3. Are you weighting intelligence the same way for this hire as you did for your first ten, or has the test changed?
   - Push until: naming the point in seniority or company stage where the deciding trait shifted from raw ability toward energy and drive.
   - Red flags: a fixed rubric applied identically at every level regardless of role or stage.
4. What are you building that doesn't look fundable yet, and what convinced you before anyone else agreed?
   - Push until: the specific signal that moved them before consensus caught up — a capability, a cost curve, an early user, not a trend.
   - Red flags: entering a category that's already crowded with funded competitors and calling that validation.
5. What would it actually take to remove your current bottleneck, and who has already solved this for themselves?
   - Push until: a concrete piece of infrastructure, deal or process change that would remove the constraint, not a wish.
   - Red flags: assuming the bottleneck resolves itself as the company grows.
6. If you had to defend one decision you made on a hunch before the data existed to back it up, what was it and what did you see that others didn't?
   - Push until: a specific bet made ahead of consensus, with what was visible to them at the time.
   - Red flags: describing a decision as obvious in hindsight with no account of the uncertainty at the time.

## Grounding

- Co-founded Greplin, renamed Cue, which Apple acquired on October 3, 2013 for a reported $40–60 million before shutting the product down; Gross then joined Apple as a machine learning director [S1].
- Joined Y Combinator as a partner in 2017, focused on AI [S2].
- Built AI Grant with Nat Friedman starting in 2017 to give no-strings grants to people working on interesting AI problems, aiming to find non-traditional machine learning engineers who lacked credentials such as a Stanford PhD [S2].
- Identified compute, not funding, as the actual bottleneck facing AI startups, and with Friedman built the Andromeda Cluster, a roughly $100 million GPU supercomputer that launched in 2023 with 2,512 Nvidia H100 GPUs and grew past 4,000 GPUs by 2024, to give portfolio companies direct access [S3].
- Co-founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. in June 2024 with Ilya Sutskever and Daniel Levy and served as its CEO [S4].
- Left Safe Superintelligence on June 29, 2025; Ilya Sutskever announced the departure and took over as CEO, while Gross joined Meta's newly launched Meta Superintelligence Labs [S4].
- As of January 2026 co-leads Meta's "Meta Compute" initiative with Meta's head of global infrastructure Santosh Janardhan, in a group Mark Zuckerberg said is "responsible for long-term capacity strategy, supplier partnerships, industry analysis, planning, and business modeling" [S5].
- Co-authored the book *Talent: How to Identify Energizers, Creatives, and Winners Around the World* with economist Tyler Cowen, published in 2022 [S6].
- Says "a great emotion to be on the lookout for in an interview, in particular when assessing founders, is fear," and calls it "a very promising sign" [S6].
- Says that as a person becomes more senior, "you want to switch to caring about just how vigorous and how energetic that person is," while adding "we're certainly not saying that horsepower and IQ don't matter" [S6].
