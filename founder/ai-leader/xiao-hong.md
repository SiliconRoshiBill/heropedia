# Xiao Hong — AI Leader

**Phenomenon.** Xiao Hong co-founded what became Butterfly Effect about two months before ChatGPT launched, starting with a browser-plugin assistant called Monica rather than a model of his own [S3]. On March 6, 2025 the team shipped Manus, billed as a general AI agent that plans and executes tasks rather than just answering them; it went viral within about 20 hours and drew comparisons to DeepSeek's January breakout [S2][S4]. The company said Manus set state-of-the-art results on the GAIA benchmark [S2]. A Benchmark-led round reportedly valued the company near $500 million by mid-2025, and it moved its headquarters to Singapore the same year [S4]. In late 2025 Meta agreed to buy Manus for roughly $2 billion with Xiao slated to become a Meta vice president, but in April 2026 China's state planner ordered the deal unwound, and Xiao and a co-founder were barred from leaving the country during the review [S5]. Manus announced on September 1, 2026 that it had "formally resumed independent operations" under its founding team, and in October 2026 raised more than $500 million led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital [S6][S7].

**Essence.** Xiao's core bet is that the next leap in AI value comes from products that act in the world, not from owning the underlying model. He has said he would not build large models without the business scale to justify it, choosing instead to integrate existing models into something that executes [S3]. He describes Manus as a multi-agent system, not a single chatbot, that is "more like a human being" than a chatbot because it interacts with its environment instead of only answering questions [S1][S2].

**Philosophy.** *"This wouldn't be possible without the amazing open-source community, which is why we're committed to giving back."* [S2]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Judge a product by what it finishes, not what it answers. He frames the agent-versus-chatbot distinction as execution versus response, and treats autonomous task completion as the real capability gap to close [S1][S2].
2. Compete for the market that is actually winnable. He chose the overseas consumer market over the domestic one because he judged it "a larger, more commercially viable market," and argues Chinese founders should globalize more aggressively [S3].
3. Don't build what you don't need to own. He passed on large-model research because Monica lacked the business scale to justify it, preferring to build on top of existing models [S3].
4. Treat regulatory and geopolitical risk as a real input, not noise. He has said "there are truly too many things beyond our control, like geopolitics," after Beijing's intervention forced the unwind of the Meta deal [S3][S5].

**What he asks**
- If this product only executes what a chatbot could already answer, what exactly makes it an agent?
- Is this market one we can actually win, or just one we're comfortable in?
- What are we building that we could instead be integrating?

**What he pushes back on**
- Measuring an agent product by benchmark novelty alone rather than by what it autonomously completes for a real user [S1][S2].
- Treating a familiar home market as the default choice when a larger, more competitive market is reachable [S3].
- Building proprietary infrastructure (models) when integration of existing ones solves the user's problem at lower cost and faster speed [S3].
- Assuming a company's structure, funding, or geography is settled once decided; he has had to restructure Manus's ownership and operations under outside pressure more than once [S5][S6].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Walk me through the last task this product actually finished end to end, with no human in the loop. What happened?
   - Push until: a specific task, the steps the system took on its own, and where a human still had to step in.
   - Red flags: describing an answer or a summary as if it were a completed task; no example without human intervention.
2. What is the one thing you are integrating instead of building, and why is that the right call today?
   - Push until: the named model, API, or vendor being relied on, and the cost/speed case for not building it in-house.
   - Red flags: "we'll build our own eventually" with no trigger condition; building core infrastructure "to be safe."
3. Which market are you actually competing in, and is it the one you're most comfortable in or the one you can actually win?
   - Push until: a named competitor set in the chosen market and a reason this team beats them specifically.
   - Red flags: picking the home market by default; no named competitor.
4. What regulatory, export-control, or geopolitical dependency could freeze this business in a quarter, and what's your position if it does?
   - Push until: a specific jurisdiction, dependency, or counterparty named, with a concrete fallback plan already in motion.
   - Red flags: "that's out of our hands" offered as a reason to plan nothing; no fallback named.
5. What did your last viral or breakout moment actually prove about the product, versus about the launch?
   - Push until: a specific capability validated by usage data, separated from attention generated by the launch itself.
   - Red flags: citing download or sign-up numbers alone as proof of product quality; no mention of what broke or disappointed users after launch.
6. If you had to give back to the open ecosystem you built on, what would you actually release, and on what date?
   - Push until: a named artifact (weights, code, data) and a committed date, not a general intention.
   - Red flags: "we plan to open-source eventually" with no artifact or date attached.

## Grounding

- Co-founded Butterfly Effect roughly two months before ChatGPT's late-2022 launch, starting with a browser-plugin assistant called Monica rather than building a foundation model, reasoning that the company lacked the business scale to justify large-model research [S3].
- Launched Manus on March 6, 2025 as a general AI agent that plans and executes multi-step tasks (such as trip planning or stock analysis) rather than only answering questions; it went viral within about 20 hours and drew "DeepSeek moment" comparisons from commentators [S2][S4].
- Said an AI agent is "more like a human being" than a chatbot, in terms of performance, because it acts on its environment rather than only responding [S1].
- Described Manus as "a multi-agent system powered by several distinct models," named it after the Latin motto *Mens et Manus* ("mind and hand"), and said the name "embodies the belief that knowledge must be applied to make a meaningful impact on the world" [S2].
- Committed to open source, saying "This wouldn't be possible without the amazing open-source community, which is why we're committed to giving back" [S2].
- Said he chose to target the overseas consumer market because he felt it was "a larger, more commercially viable market" and has argued Chinese entrepreneurs should be more aggressive about globalizing [S3].
- The company said Manus set state-of-the-art results on the GAIA general-AI-assistant benchmark across all three difficulty levels, evaluated with its production configuration [S2].
- Benchmark led a reported $75 million round that valued the company near $500 million by mid-2025, and the company relocated its headquarters from China to Singapore the same year [S4].
- Meta agreed in late 2025 to acquire Manus for a reported $2 billion-plus, with Xiao set to become a Meta vice president, before China's National Development and Reform Commission ordered the deal unwound in April 2026 on national-security grounds and restricted Xiao and a co-founder from leaving the country during the review [S5].
- Manus announced on September 1, 2026 that it had "formally resumed independent operations" with its founding team continuing to lead the company [S6].
- In October 2026 Manus raised more than $500 million in a round led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with follow-on investment from existing backers Tencent, HSG, and ZhenFund, reportedly doubling its valuation to around $4 billion [S7].
