# Zhang Peng — AI Leader

**Phenomenon.** Zhang Peng co-founded Zhipu AI (智谱AI, later branded internationally as Z.ai) in June 2019 as a spinout of Tsinghua University's Knowledge Engineering Group lab, alongside chief scientist Tang Jie and chairman Liu Debing [S4]. In 2023 the company raised more than 2.5 billion yuan (about $342 million) from Alibaba, Tencent, Ant Group, Xiaomi, Meituan and HongShan [S3]. On January 8, 2026, Zhang took Zhipu AI/Z.ai (listed as Knowledge Atlas Technology Joint Stock Co.) public on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange at a valuation near HK$55.5 billion (US$7.1 billion), after the company's planned mainland listing stalled [S2].

**Essence.** Zhang runs open weights and a paid API as two tracks of one business rather than a single choice: Zhipu released the GLM-4 and GLM-Z1 model series under the MIT license while selling the same lineage through its commercial Model-as-a-Service platform and the international Z.ai site [S5]. He is openly skeptical of superintelligence timelines more aggressive than his own, arguing the term is too vague to pin to a date, and he has said Zhipu competes with firms like OpenAI for enterprise customers while it is not yet fighting for US consumer subscriptions [S1]. He also treats a sudden competitive shock, such as DeepSeek's early-2025 breakthrough, as a forcing function: he said it had a significant impact across Zhipu's research, engineering and market work and pushed the company toward a more open stance [S6].

**Philosophy.** *"Achieving or exceeding human intelligence levels by 2030 might mean surpassing humans in one or several aspects."* [S1]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Treat open-sourcing and monetization as parallel tracks, not a contradiction. The same GLM lineage that ships as open weights also funds the business through the paid API [S5].
2. Benchmark against a named rival's actual claim, not a vague forecast. He answers OpenAI's and SoftBank's superintelligence timelines with a specific, narrower claim of his own rather than a flat denial [S1].
3. Read a competitor's breakthrough as an instruction, not just a threat. DeepSeek's rise triggered company-wide discussion that he says reshaped research and market posture, not just messaging [S6].
4. Separate who builds the technology from who sells it. He describes the three co-founders' roles as split by function — technology, capital, commercialization — rather than concentrated in one person [S4].

**What he asks**
- Which specific capability or benchmark would have to move, and by how much, before a timeline forecast should change?
- What did a competitor's breakthrough actually change in our roadmap this quarter, not just in our talking points?
- Where exactly are we competing for a named customer against a named rival, versus just being compared to them in the press?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating "superintelligence" as a single, datable event rather than a basket of separate capabilities that arrive at different times [S1].
- Casting open-source and commercial models as mutually exclusive strategies, when Zhipu runs both from the same model family [S5].
- Responding to a competitive shock with messaging alone instead of a changed research or market decision [S6].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Which of your models ship as open weights, which stay behind the paid API, and what rule decided that split for the last model you released?
   - Push until: a named model, the criterion that placed it (size, commercial tier, strategic value), and who made that call.
   - Red flags: "it depends"; no specific model named; claiming everything is open while a flagship stays closed with no stated reason.
2. When a rival undercuts your price or benchmark overnight, what in your roadmap actually changed, not just your messaging?
   - Push until: one concrete decision reversed or accelerated within a stated window — a release date moved, a price cut, a research direction dropped.
   - Red flags: "we always planned that"; "we had internal discussions" with no decision named; nothing changed but the talking points.
3. Who funded your last round, what claim did they get on the company, and what do they expect back, and by when?
   - Push until: the investor named, the instrument (equity, board seat, revenue commitment), and the date they are holding you to.
   - Red flags: "strategic investors" with no names; no mention of what they expect in return; treating state-linked capital the same as ordinary venture money with no distinction.
4. You call today's superintelligence forecasts too vague to date — what single measured result would make you change that answer?
   - Push until: one named benchmark or deployed task, today's score on it, and the score that would flip the forecast.
   - Red flags: restating the skepticism without naming a threshold; treating the question as unanswerable.
5. Of the capital you just raised, what fraction is committed to compute, to people, and to anything else, and who signed off on that split?
   - Push until: a percentage breakdown and the name of the executive or board member accountable for each line.
   - Red flags: "it's all for R&D" with no breakdown; no named owner of the allocation.
6. Where exactly does Zhipu compete with OpenAI today — which account, which deal, which deliverable — versus where you are simply being compared to them?
   - Push until: a specific customer or deal won or lost against a named competitor, with the reason it went that way.
   - Red flags: citing media rankings or comparisons instead of an actual competitive deal; no customer named.

## Grounding

- Co-founded Zhipu AI (智谱AI, later branded internationally as Z.ai) in June 2019 as a spinout of Tsinghua University's Knowledge Engineering Group lab, together with chief scientist Tang Jie and chairman Liu Debing, after earning a 2005 master's degree at the same lab [S4].
- Took the commercialization role among the three co-founders, while Tang Jie leads technology and Liu Debing leads capital operations; company insiders describe the trio as sharing a "First Principles" approach [S4].
- Raised more than 2.5 billion yuan (about $342 million) in 2023 from investors including Alibaba, Tencent, Ant Group, Xiaomi, Meituan and HongShan [S3].
- Took Zhipu AI/Z.ai (listed as Knowledge Atlas Technology Joint Stock Co.) through its Hong Kong Stock Exchange debut on January 8, 2026, where shares opened above the HK$116.20 offer price and the company was valued at roughly HK$55.5 billion (US$7.1 billion); about 70% of the HK$4.17 billion raised was earmarked for AI model R&D through 2028 [S2].
- Open-sourced the GLM-4 and GLM-Z1 model series under the MIT license while continuing to sell the same model lineage through Zhipu's paid Model-as-a-Service API, and launched the international Z.ai site to broaden global access [S5].
- Said the concept of superintelligence is too vague to tie to a specific date, arguing that achieving or exceeding human intelligence levels by 2030 might mean surpassing humans in one or several aspects, but likely still falling far short in many areas [S1].
- Called OpenAI's description of Zhipu as a fast-rising rival "flattered," said the company's overseas expansion was simply normal business, and said Zhipu competes with firms like OpenAI for enterprise clients while not yet competing with US models in consumer subscriptions [S1].
- Said DeepSeek's early-2025 breakthrough had a significant impact across Zhipu's research, engineering and market work, and that internal discussions held right after the Spring Festival pushed the company toward a more open stance on both its model research and its market approach [S6].
