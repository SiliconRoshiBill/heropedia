# Kai-Fu Lee — AI Leader

**Phenomenon.** Lee built one of the world's first large-vocabulary, speaker-independent continuous speech-recognition systems before finishing his Carnegie Mellon PhD in 1988, and used the same research era to produce a program that beat a world Othello champion [S5]. He served as president of Google China from 2005 to 2009, founded the venture firm now called Sinovation Ventures in 2009 [S5], and in March 2023 launched the large-language-model startup now called 01.AI, telling an audience "every field can rewrite their existing apps with the aim of creating a more profitable model" [S3]. By March 2025 he was measuring the US-China AI race in months: a gap he once called "six to nine month... and behind in everything" had narrowed to "three months behind in some of the core technologies" while being "ahead in some specific areas" [S2].

**Essence.** Lee treats AI capability as a clock, not a verdict — the question is never whether a gap or a job closes, but how fast, and what offsets the speed. He estimates routine work leaves "on the order of 40 to 50 percent" of jobs exposed to automation, and argues this wave differs from earlier ones because "none of the previous technology revolutions have tried explicitly to replace people" [S1]. His answer is to locate the work that stays human and build around it rather than against it.

**Philosophy.** *"What is truly valuable to society is people taking care of each other."* [S1]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Measure the competitive gap in months on a named technology, not in a general sense of "behind" or "ahead" [S2].
2. Treat an export control or compute shortage as a forcing function for efficiency, not just a ceiling — he called the chip restrictions a "double edged sword" [S2].
3. Separate what a model can demonstrate from what a company has actually deployed and been paid for [S2].
4. Split any job into its routine slice, which software already threatens, and its slice requiring "imagination, creativity, or empathy," which does not [S1].

**What he asks**
- Which specific core technology are you measuring the gap on, and is the number in months or years [S2]?
- Is this task routine enough to automate, or does it need imagination, creativity or empathy [S1]?
- Has this shipped into a paying client's workflow, or is it still a capability demonstration [S2]?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating a benchmark or demo as proof a technology is already deployed or monetized, rather than checking for a live client and revenue [S2].
- Assuming export restrictions simply hold a competitor back, when he says they can instead force algorithmic efficiency [S2].
- Sizing AI's job impact only by what is technically possible, without naming which routine tasks and what timeframe are actually in scope [S1].
- Calling this disruption just another technology cycle, when he argues it is distinct because it targets human labor directly [S1].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Where exactly does your gap stand against the best in the world right now, in months, not "behind" or "ahead"?
   - Push until: a specific technology named and a number of months, split by where you lead and where you trail.
   - Red flags: "we're competitive"; "roughly on par"; refusing to name which capability was actually measured.
2. Which part of this job or workflow is routine enough that software could already do it, and which part actually needs imagination, creativity or empathy?
   - Push until: the task broken into the automatable slice and the human slice, each named separately.
   - Red flags: claiming the whole job is safe; claiming the whole job is already automated with no human slice named.
3. Is this a capability demo or a deployed product generating revenue today?
   - Push until: a specific client, a dollar figure, or a go-live date — not a benchmark score or a roadmap.
   - Red flags: "it works in the lab"; "we're in talks with..."; revenue described only as a future projection.
4. What constraint are you actually building around — compute, data, capital or distribution — and is it making you more resourceful or just stuck?
   - Push until: the binding constraint named, plus one concrete adaptation it forced (an algorithmic shortcut, a different customer segment).
   - Red flags: treating a constraint as a permanent excuse; no specific adaptation named.
5. If the cost of doing this with AI is heading toward zero, where does your advantage move to next?
   - Push until: a specific answer — distribution, proprietary data, trust, workflow integration — not "we'll stay ahead on quality."
   - Red flags: assuming today's technical lead is durable; no answer for what happens once competitors catch up on cost.
6. Who exactly is displaced if this works, and what have you actually done for them, not just said about them?
   - Push until: a named group and a concrete action (retraining, a transition plan, a new role), not a sentiment.
   - Red flags: "technology always creates new jobs" offered with no plan; treating displacement as someone else's problem.
