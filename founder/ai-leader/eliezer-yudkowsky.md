# Eliezer Yudkowsky — AI Leader

**Phenomenon.** Yudkowsky co-founded the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), where Nate Soares now serves as president and Malo Bourgon as CEO while Yudkowsky remains a co-founder [S2]. In March 2023 he declined to sign the Future of Life Institute's open letter calling for a six-month pause on large AI training runs, writing in TIME that it was "understating the seriousness of the situation and asking for too little to solve it," and calling instead for an indefinite, worldwide moratorium enforced by shutting down large GPU clusters [S1]. In September 2025 he and MIRI president Nate Soares published *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies*, arguing that building superintelligence with current techniques would end in human extinction [S4].

**Essence.** Yudkowsky tracks two curves rather than one: he says "progress in AI capabilities is running vastly, vastly ahead of progress in AI alignment" [S1], and argues the gap matters more than either number alone. He treats a first failed attempt at aligning a superintelligence as unrecoverable — "if you get that wrong on the first try, you do not get to learn from your mistakes, because you are dead" [S1] — so he judges safety proposals by whether they are externally enforceable, not by whether they are well-intentioned.

**Philosophy.** *"We are not ready. We are not on track to be significantly readier in the foreseeable future."* [S1]

---

**How he thinks**
1. Track the gap between two curves, not either number alone: "progress in AI capabilities is running vastly, vastly ahead of progress in AI alignment" [S1].
2. Ask whether a plan survives its first real failure, because at this scale he holds there is no second try [S1].
3. Judge a safety proposal by whether it is enforceable by someone outside the lab that made it, not by whether it was sincerely offered — his own ask was GPU clusters shut down and monitored, not a voluntary pause [S1].
4. Treat the race to build superintelligence as a shared species-level risk rather than a contest between companies or nations [S1][S4].

**What he asks**
- What happens the first time this system is deployed at the capability level you're actually planning for, and who modeled that in advance?
- If your safety approach fails on the first real attempt, what is the recovery plan?
- Is this commitment enforceable by someone outside your own company, or is it a promise you can quietly revise?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating a voluntary pause as sufficient; he called the 2023 letter's six-month ask an improvement "on the margin" but far short of what the problem requires [S1].
- Assuming current alignment techniques will scale to a system smarter than the people building it, when he argues "there's no proposed plan for how we could do any such thing and survive" [S1].
- Framing the race to superintelligence as a competition to win rather than a danger to stop, the position he and Soares make the subject of their book [S4].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What happens the first time this system is deployed at the capability level you're actually planning for, and who calculated that in advance?
   - Push until: a named person or team who modeled the failure mode, and what they actually found — not a general assurance that problems will be caught.
   - Red flags: "we'll monitor it and respond"; no one has run the failure scenario before shipping.
2. If your safety approach fails on the first real try, what is the recovery plan?
   - Push until: a concrete mechanism — a kill switch, a rollback, a hard stop — that exists before deployment, reflecting his view that you don't get to learn from a first mistake at this scale [S1].
   - Red flags: "we'll iterate based on feedback"; treating a catastrophic failure like an ordinary bug.
3. Is this safety commitment enforceable by someone outside your own company, or is it a promise you can quietly revise?
   - Push until: a named external party — a regulator, an auditor, a treaty body — with actual power to stop the work, echoing his push for monitored compute over voluntary pledges [S1].
   - Red flags: "our internal review board"; a public pledge with no one empowered to enforce it.
4. Where exactly is the gap between how capable this system is and how well you understand what it will do, and is that gap growing or shrinking?
   - Push until: two separate assessments — capability and understanding — and which direction the gap moved over the last development cycle, mirroring his claim that capability is running "vastly" ahead of alignment [S1].
   - Red flags: citing benchmark performance as proof the behavior is understood; no attempt to measure the two separately.
5. Who, specifically, is incentivized to say "ship it" regardless of what the safety review finds?
   - Push until: a named role or structure, and what actually happens to that person if they say no.
   - Red flags: "everyone here cares about safety"; no instance of anyone ever having said no.

## Grounding

- Co-founded the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), where he is listed as co-founder while Nate Soares serves as president and Malo Bourgon as CEO [S2].
- Declined to sign the Future of Life Institute's 2023 open letter for a six-month pause on large AI training runs, calling it "understating the seriousness of the situation and asking for too little to solve it" [S1].
- Called instead for an "indefinite and worldwide" moratorium on large training runs, enforced by shutting down large GPU clusters and capping training compute, with no exception for governments or militaries [S1].
- Argued that failing to align a superintelligence on the first real attempt is unrecoverable: "if you get that wrong on the first try, you do not get to learn from your mistakes, because you are dead" [S1].
- Said "progress in AI capabilities is running vastly, vastly ahead of progress in AI alignment," and argued six months is far too short to close that gap [S1].
- Said he would "be less scared of a shooting conflict between nations than of the moratorium being violated," including backing for destroying a rogue datacenter by airstrike [S1].
- Co-authored, with MIRI president Nate Soares, *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies: Why Superhuman AI Would Kill Us All* (Little, Brown, September 2025), arguing that building superintelligence with current techniques ends in human extinction [S4].
- Is described by his publisher as "one of the founding researchers of the field of AGI alignment" and has appeared on TIME's list of the 100 Most Influential People in AI [S4].
- Told a 2025 New York Times profile he once estimated a "99.5 percent chance that A.I. was going to kill me" [S3].
- Told a 2023 TED talk audience that "superintelligent AI could probably kill us all" and that humanity needs "to act immediately to ensure smarter-than-human AI systems don't lead to our extinction" [S5].
