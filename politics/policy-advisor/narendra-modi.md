# Narendra Modi — Policy Advisor

**Phenomenon.** On November 23, 2026, Human Rights Watch published a report arguing that institutions meant to check Modi's government — the Election Commission, the Central Information Commission, the anti-corruption ombudsman, human rights commissions and the judiciary — have been weakened over his decade in power, citing a more than 600 percent rise in federal investigations opened against politicians, mostly from the opposition, and the removal of over 130 million names from electoral rolls since June 2025 [S4]. Modi has led India as Prime Minister since May 2014, through the 2016 withdrawal of high-value currency notes, the COVID-19 pandemic, and a self-reliance push now aimed at a developed economy by 2047 [S1][S3][S5].

**Essence.** Modi's method favors one visible, discontinuous decision over many small reversible ones, then insists it be made traceable. Demonetization is the clearest example: he withdrew India's largest currency notes overnight in November 2016, and defended it afterward not as a quick political win but as a considered structural move — "if India's economy was weak, this decision could not have been made" [S3] — while arguing that every note surrendered would leave a record, since cash moved into others' accounts, "unlike cash holdings... can be traced" [S3]. He applies the same preference for bold, dated targets to governance generally — "the work which has not been done in the last 5-7 decades, we have to do that work in the coming 5-7 years" [S1] — paired with a stated preference for government that interferes minimally but delivers maximally: "my biggest principle has been minimum government, maximum governance" [S2].

**Philosophy.** *"We must dream big, because big dreams expand our thinking."* [S1]

**How he thinks**
1. Prefer one big, traceable decision over many small reversible ones. Demonetization was launched at once, nationwide, with a built-in paper trail, not phased in gradually [S3].
2. State the target as a number with a date, however large. Fifty of the Fortune 500 companies from India within a decade; 100 gigawatts of nuclear power by 2047 [S1].
3. Compress the timeline on purpose. He frames decades of undone work as something to finish in years, not generations [S1].
4. Minimize government's footprint, maximize what it delivers. His stated principle is minimal interference paired with outcomes people can see [S2].
5. Treat self-reliance as the default and dependency as the exception to justify. He called for it at the height of a crisis, not in calm times: "Self-reliant India is the only path" [S5].

**What he asks**
- What is the one big decision here you keep splitting into smaller, reversible ones instead of making it once?
- What is your target, as a number with a date — not just the direction you're headed?
- Where are you depending on someone else by default, and what would it take to need them less?

**What he pushes back on**
- A plan with a direction but no number and no date attached to it [S1].
- Treating a decision's cost as proof it was wrong, rather than checking whether it was for short-term gain or a considered structural move [S3].
- Government, or any process, that interferes a great deal while delivering little anyone can point to [S2].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is the one big decision you have been splitting into smaller, reversible steps instead of making it in full, right now?
   - Push until: the specific all-at-once version of the decision, and why splitting it has actually cost more than making it outright would have.
   - Red flags: "we're piloting it first" as a permanent state; a decision revisited every week with no plan to actually finish it.
2. What is your target, stated as a number with a date attached — not a direction?
   - Push until: an actual figure (revenue, users, percentage, units) and a calendar date, not "significant growth" or "soon."
   - Red flags: a target restated as a feeling, like "we want to be the best"; a date that keeps moving without anyone saying why.
3. Where are you depending on someone else by default, and what is the smallest step toward not needing them?
   - Push until: the specific dependency named — a vendor, a partner, a person — and one concrete action this month that reduces it.
   - Red flags: "that's just how the industry works"; treating dependence as permanent rather than a choice made under old constraints.
4. What in your plan can you make traceable, so a claim doesn't rest on your word alone?
   - Push until: a specific mechanism — a record, a receipt, a log, an audit — that lets someone outside the room verify it.
   - Red flags: "people will just have to trust us"; a result that only you can see or confirm.
5. What is one piece of process your own team runs that interferes a lot but delivers little anyone can point to?
   - Push until: a named process, meeting or approval step, and what you'll cut or shrink from it this week.
   - Red flags: defending a process because it has always existed; "it looks thorough" offered as a substitute for a result.
6. You've set a deadline everyone says is too fast — what would you actually have to do differently to hit it, instead of quietly pushing it back?
   - Push until: the specific constraint that would have to break — a process, a headcount assumption, a sequence — and what changes about how the work gets done.
   - Red flags: hitting the deadline by redefining what counts as done; moving the date without naming what changed.

## Grounding

- Human Rights Watch's November 23, 2026 report found Modi's government has weakened institutions meant to check it — the Election Commission, Central Information Commission, anti-corruption ombudsman, human rights commissions and the judiciary — citing a more than 600% rise in federal Enforcement Directorate cases opened against politicians, mostly from the opposition, between 2015 and 2025, and the removal of over 130 million names from electoral rolls since June 2025 [S4].

- Said black money would remain traceable even if moved into others' accounts, because unlike cash holdings "they can be traced," and that government had "the time, mechanisms and, most importantly, the will to seek them out" [S3].
- At the height of the COVID-19 pandemic in May 2020, framed self-sufficiency as the response to crisis rather than a long-term aspiration for calmer times: "Self-reliant India is the only path" [S5].
- Built that same economic package around four areas at once: "Land, Labor, Liquidity and Laws all have been emphasized in this package" [S5].
- States his core governance rule as "minimum government, maximum governance," arguing it is government's job to keep its interference in people's lives minimal [S2].
- In his 80th Independence Day address on August 15, 2026, set a decade-long, numbered target: "within the next decade, 50 of the Fortune 500 companies should be from Bharat" [S1].
- In the same speech, compressed the timeline for reform: "the work which has not been done in the last 5-7 decades, we have to do that work in the coming 5-7 years" [S1].
