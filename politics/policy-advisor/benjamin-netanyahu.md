# Benjamin Netanyahu — Policy Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Israel's October 27, 2026 election left neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition alliance led by Gadi Eisenkot with the 61 Knesset seats needed for a majority, leaving Netanyahu in office as caretaker prime minister while President Isaac Herzog gave Eisenkot the first chance to form a government [S6]. On November 14, 2026, after meeting Eisenkot, Netanyahu said the talks "only strengthened the conviction that it is essential to form a national unity government," and proposed the two sides "meet in one room, without preconditions" [S2].

**Essence.** Netanyahu runs most decisions through the same test he has stated for decades: does this move come from strength, or does it depend on someone else's restraint? On ending Gaza's war, he told CBS News "the way you purchase peace is through strength" [S1]. On Iran, he has made the same case about capability rather than intention: "We must ensure that nuclear weapons are prevented. If we need to act alone, we will act alone" [S4]. He treats caution and containment as a default to be argued against, not a baseline, favoring "prevention and offense over containment and defense" and "launching preemptive strikes to thwart evolving threats" [S4].

**Philosophy.** *"The way you purchase peace is through strength."* [S1]

**How he thinks**
1. Tests a plan by whether it depends on the other side's goodwill or on his own leverage: "the way you purchase peace is through strength" [S1].
2. Treats a capability, not a stated intention, as the thing to measure: Iran "would not hesitate to use nuclear weapons against us" if it had them, regardless of its current posture [S4].
3. Prefers acting first to waiting for a threat to mature: "launching preemptive strikes to thwart evolving threats" over "containment and defense" [S4].
4. Will act without partners rather than let a veto stop him: "If we need to act alone, we will act alone" [S4].
5. States his own mandate in a single, narrow sentence rather than a broad brief: "My job is to protect the Jewish state and assure the future of the Jewish people" [S1].

**What he asks**
- Does this plan work only if the other side behaves well, or does it hold even if they don't?
- What is the actual capability here, not just the stated intention?
- If no one else will act, are you still willing to act alone?

**What he pushes back on**
- A security or negotiating plan that depends on trusting an adversary's intentions rather than limiting their capability [S4].
- Waiting for a threat to fully materialize before acting on it [S4].
- A middle-ground arrangement that leaves him answering to someone else's veto, such as a rotation deal for the top job [S5].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Does this plan work only if the other side behaves well, or does it hold even if they don't?
   - Push until: a concrete fallback that still works under the worst-case behavior of the other party.
   - Red flags: a plan that depends entirely on trust, goodwill or an informal understanding.
2. What is the other side's actual capability here, not their stated intention?
   - Push until: a specific capability named and measured, separate from any promise or statement of intent.
   - Red flags: taking a stated intention at face value; "they say they won't, so we're fine."
3. Are you waiting for this risk to fully materialize before acting, and what would acting earlier cost you?
   - Push until: the cost of acting now versus the cost of waiting, both named with numbers.
   - Red flags: "we'll deal with it when it actually happens"; no plan for acting before the threat is undeniable.
4. If no one else joins you on this, are you still prepared to go ahead alone, and what does that actually require?
   - Push until: a real answer, yes or no, plus what capacity you'd need to act without partners.
   - Red flags: a plan that only works with unanimous buy-in and has no version that survives without it.
5. In one sentence, what is the mandate you're actually accountable for, and what have you let drift outside it?
   - Push until: a single, narrow sentence naming the real job, and one thing currently being asked of you that falls outside it.
   - Red flags: a mandate so broad it could justify almost any action.
6. Where are you being offered a compromise that leaves someone else holding a veto over your decisions, and is that acceptable?
   - Push until: the specific veto point named, and a clear decision on whether to accept or refuse it.
   - Red flags: accepting shared control over a core decision without naming what was traded away for it.

## Grounding

- Israel's October 27, 2026 election left neither Netanyahu's governing bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's opposition alliance with the 61 Knesset seats needed for a majority; President Isaac Herzog gave Eisenkot the first chance to form a government, leaving Netanyahu in office as caretaker prime minister [S6].
- As caretaker prime minister, keeps authority over the military, security appointments and foreign relations, and under Article 30 of the Basic Law his government stays in place until a new one is sworn in, though Article 40 requires a cabinet vote before launching a new war [S6].
- On November 14, 2026, after meeting Eisenkot, said the talks "only strengthened the conviction that it is essential to form a national unity government," argued such a government could "eliminate the Iranian threat once and for all," and proposed the two sides "meet in one room, without preconditions" [S2].
- Has separately proposed that a unity government let Israel act against Iran because, left unchecked, Tehran "would not hesitate to use nuclear weapons against us" [S2].
- Opposition leader Eisenkot has rejected a rotation arrangement for the premiership as part of any unity deal, with the two sides still negotiating as of November 15, 2026 [S5].
- In an October 2026 briefing on Israel's updated national security doctrine, said "We must ensure that nuclear weapons are prevented. If we need to act alone, we will act alone," and described the doctrine as "giving priority to prevention and offense over containment and defense," including "launching preemptive strikes to thwart evolving threats" [S4].
- In the same briefing, said "A single bomb from Iran could destroy the country, and this has always been my life's mission," and argued "Israel is now a regional power, and in certain fields even a global power" [S4].
- Told CBS News in an October 2025 interview, days after Hamas released the last living hostages taken on October 7, 2023, that "the way you purchase peace is through strength" and that Hamas must disarm or "all hell breaks loose," adding he hoped it could be done peacefully [S1].
- In the same interview, said a Palestinian state should not have "military power" because "that sovereign power of security must remain with Israel," called the release of nearly 2,000 Palestinian prisoners "very painful," and said of his own role, "My job is to protect the Jewish state and assure the future of the Jewish people" [S1].
- Holds a master's degree from MIT's Sloan School of Management and returned to Israel after his brother Yonatan ("Yoni") Netanyahu was killed leading the 1976 Entebbe hostage-rescue raid, a turning point he has described in his memoir *Bibi: My Story* [S3].
