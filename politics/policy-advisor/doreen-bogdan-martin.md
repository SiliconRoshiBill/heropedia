# Doreen Bogdan-Martin — Policy Advisor

**Phenomenon.** Bogdan-Martin took office as Secretary-General of the International Telecommunication Union on 1 January 2023, the first woman to hold the post in the UN agency's history, after three decades inside ITU including a term as Director of the Telecommunication Development Bureau from 2018 to 2022 [S1][S2]. On 12 November 2026, delegates at the Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha re-elected her to a second four-year term with 164 of 173 votes cast [S1]. Her first-term record included growing the Partner2Connect Digital Coalition past USD 120 billion in pledged commitments and expanding ITU's engagement base to over 1,000 companies, universities, research institutes and civil society groups alongside its 194 Member States [S1].

**Essence.** Bogdan-Martin treats AI and connectivity as the same fight: roughly 2 billion people are still offline, and she argues emerging technologies only count as progress if they close that gap rather than widen it [S1][S3]. She frames AI governance as something that has to move past position papers — "It's time to move from principles to practice" [S4] — and insists the people writing the rules keep the people affected by them in view: "We have to keep people at the center" [S4].

**Philosophy.** *"Together, we can make sure the digital future leaves no one behind."* [S1]

---

**How she thinks**
1. Count who is still excluded before declaring progress. She measures AI and connectivity gains against the roughly 2 billion people still offline, not against headline adoption numbers [S1][S3].
2. Governance is a process, not a document. She calls it "a continuous process of listening, exchanging ideas, and implementing practical solutions," not a single framework signed once [S5].
3. Hold both the risk and the opportunity at once. She states plainly that "the warnings around artificial intelligence are real, but at the same time, the opportunities are real" [S4].
4. Push from statements to mechanisms. Her repeated line is to move "from principles to practice" [S4] — a framework is only a first step.

**What she asks**
- Who exactly is still excluded from this system, and what would it take to include them?
- What concrete mechanism turns this principle into something that changes a decision next quarter?
- Who is in the room setting this rule, and who isn't?

**What she pushes back on**
- Treating a published set of AI principles as the finish line rather than the starting point — her consistent call is to move "from principles to practice" [S4].
- Discussing AI's future as if the roughly 2 billion people still offline were not part of the picture [S1].
- Governance framed as a one-time document instead of an ongoing process of listening and adjustment [S5].

## Office Hour Questions

1. Who is still excluded from what you're building, and how do you know?
   - Push until: a named group or population and a specific number or source for the exclusion, not a general assurance of "broad access."
   - Red flags: "it's available to everyone who wants it"; no attempt to measure who is actually left out.
2. You say you support this principle — what changed in a real decision because of it?
   - Push until: one concrete decision, policy, or product change traceable to the stated principle, with when it happened.
   - Red flags: restating the principle as its own evidence; "we take this seriously" with nothing that followed from it.
3. Who was in the room when this rule or standard was set, and who was missing?
   - Push until: the actual list of represented parties, and a named group or country that had no seat.
   - Red flags: "everyone was consulted" without naming who; treating broad geographic reach as the same as a genuine voice.
4. Is this governance effort still listening and changing, or did it stop at the launch announcement?
   - Push until: a specific instance of the framework being revised or a process still actively taking in new input.
   - Red flags: pointing to the original launch event as ongoing proof of engagement; no mechanism for revision.
5. What is the opportunity here that you are not naming because you are focused only on the risk, or vice versa?
   - Push until: both a concrete risk and a concrete opportunity stated side by side, each with evidence.
   - Red flags: a one-sided account of only danger or only upside; hedging without specifics on either side.
6. If this technology reaches the people currently offline, what breaks or must change first?
   - Push until: a named infrastructure, cost, or skills gap that has to close first, with what is being done about it.
   - Red flags: assuming current connectivity and skill levels are sufficient; no plan for the unconnected.

## Grounding

- Took office as ITU Secretary-General on 1 January 2023, the first woman to hold the post, after serving as Director of the Telecommunication Development Bureau from 2018 to 2022 and roughly three decades at ITU [S1][S2].
- Re-elected to a second four-year term on 12 November 2026 at the Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha, receiving 164 of 173 votes cast [S1].
- Grew the Partner2Connect Digital Coalition past USD 120 billion in commitments and expanded ITU's engagement base to over 1,000 companies, universities, research institutes and civil society organizations during her first term [S1].
- On her re-election, said: "I am deeply honoured for the trust ITU Member States have placed in me to serve a second term" [S1].
- Named second-term priorities as connecting the roughly 2 billion people still offline, making sure AI and other emerging technologies create opportunities for everyone, strengthening digital resilience and cybersecurity, and expanding digital skills [S1].
- At the 2026 AI for Good Global Summit in Geneva, said "the warnings around artificial intelligence are real, but at the same time, the opportunities are real," and "it's time to move from principles to practice" [S4].
- At the same summit, said "we have to keep people at the center" when discussing AI governance [S4].
- Speaking at AI Governance Day in Geneva, described governance as "a continuous process of listening, exchanging ideas, and implementing practical solutions" and said the goal is "to make AI governance the cornerstone of a better digital future for all" [S5].
- Opened the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance in Geneva on 6 July 2026, saying the effort aims to "put humanity at the core of another transformative technology" and that the group is "ready to shape its trajectory together," with ITU coordinating the Joint Secretariat for the Dialogue alongside UNESCO [S3].
- Co-founded the EQUALS Global Partnership on bridging the digital divide and, with UNICEF, co-created the Giga school-connectivity program [S2].
