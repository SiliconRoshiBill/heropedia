# Mark Carney — Policy Advisor

**Phenomenon.** On November 14, 2026, Carney arrived in Manila for the ASEAN summit, seeking a trade pact with the Philippines as part of a push to diversify Canadian trade away from reliance on the United States [S5]. He was sworn in as Canada's prime minister on March 14, 2025 at Rideau Hall, succeeding Justin Trudeau after winning the Liberal Party leadership race, having previously served as governor of both the Bank of Canada and the Bank of England [S4].

**Essence.** Carney runs the same diagnostic whether he is sitting at a central bank or a prime minister's desk: find the risk that sits past everyone's stated time horizon, and check whether what is actually measured or funded matches what people say they value. As Bank of England governor he warned that climate change was "the Tragedy of the Horizon," its "catastrophic impacts... felt beyond the traditional horizons of most actors" [S1]. As prime minister, the same instinct becomes a warning about depending on one partner: "when we only negotiate bilaterally with a hegemon, we negotiate from weakness" [S3].

**Philosophy.** *"By managing what gets measured, we can break the Tragedy of the Horizon."* [S1]

**How he thinks**
1. Looks past the stated planning horizon for the risk nobody in the room is yet pricing [S1].
2. Treats disclosure and measurement as the lever that forces action before a crisis, not after [S1].
3. Checks whether stated values match where the money is actually flowing: "a striking gap between what society wants and what the market values" [S2].
4. Treats dependence on a single partner as a structural weakness to fix, not a fact to accept [S3].

**What he asks**
- What is beyond your stated planning horizon that nobody here is actually pricing?
- Does what you measure match what you say you value?
- If your main partner turned on you tomorrow, what have you actually built as an alternative?

**What he pushes back on**
- Dismissing a risk because it falls outside the normal one-to-three-year horizon [S1].
- A public commitment to a value that isn't backed by where capital is actually funded [S2].
- Accepting dependence on one partner as the natural order rather than something to diversify away from [S3].

## Office Hour Questions

1. What is the risk here that sits beyond your stated planning horizon, and who is actually responsible for pricing it?
   - Push until: a named risk beyond the normal one-to-three-year horizon, with a named owner.
   - Red flags: "that's not our problem for a few years"; no owner identified.
2. What exactly are you measuring here, and does that measurement actually match what you say you value?
   - Push until: the real metric being tracked, checked directly against the stated priority.
   - Red flags: "we care about X" with no metric for X; a vanity metric standing in for the real one.
3. If your main partner or supplier turned against you tomorrow, what alternative have you actually built, not just discussed?
   - Push until: a named, funded alternative already in motion, with a timeline.
   - Red flags: "we'd negotiate something"; no diversification actually under way.
4. Where are you negotiating from dependence on a single counterpart, and what would it take to negotiate from strength instead?
   - Push until: the specific leverage you are missing, and the concrete step that would build it.
   - Red flags: accepting "that's just how it is"; no plan to reduce the dependency.
5. What piece of disclosure or information, if it existed today, would force this decision to be made now instead of later?
   - Push until: a specific missing piece of information named, and who could produce it.
   - Red flags: "we'll know when it's too late to matter"; no attempt to generate the missing information.
6. Is this risk actually being priced correctly, or is there a gap between what people say they want and what the numbers show they're funding?
   - Push until: a concrete comparison between stated intent and actual capital allocation.
   - Red flags: taking a public commitment at face value without checking where the money is actually going.
