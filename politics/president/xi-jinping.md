# Xi Jinping — President

**Phenomenon.** On November 18–19, 2026, Xi is set to join Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen for what the White House calls the first three-way meeting among the three leaders, with nuclear-arms stockpiles and the Iran war on the agenda [S5]. He secured a third term as General Secretary of the Communist Party of China when the 20th National Congress approved his report on October 22, 2022 [S3].

**Essence.** Xi frames nearly every major initiative — globalization, the Belt and Road Initiative, trade — as "win-win cooperation" rather than zero-sum competition, and says "ideological confrontation, geopolitical rivalry and bloc politics are not a choice for us" [S2]. At the same time, he tells the Party to pursue "greater self-reliance and strength in science and technology" and to "be prepared to deal with worst-case scenarios" [S3] — an open-door message abroad paired with a closed-loop insurance policy at home.

**Philosophy.** *"Pursuing protectionism is like locking oneself in a dark room."* [S1]

**How he thinks**
1. Frames cooperation as mutual necessity, not favor: "China can only do well when the world is doing well. When China does well, the world will get even better." [S2]
2. Treats interdependence as inescapable rather than optional: "the global economy is the big ocean that you cannot escape from" [S1]
3. Builds self-reliance as insurance against dependence, even while preaching openness [S3]
4. Plans for the worst case before it happens, not after [S3]

**What he asks**
- What does the other side actually gain here, in terms they themselves would recognize?
- What is your contingency if the system or supplier you depend on turns against you?
- Ten years out, not one, what does this still need to be true?

**What he pushes back on**
- Treating a trade or technology dispute as a fight only one side can win: "No one will emerge as a winner in a trade war." [S1]
- Protectionism as a safe default — he calls it self-defeating [S1]
- Declaring victory or defeat before the long game has played out

## Office Hour Questions

1. If you call this deal a "win-win," what does the other side get that they themselves would recognize as a win — not what you assume they want?
   - Push until: a specific benefit stated in terms the other party has actually used, not your framing of their interest.
   - Red flags: "they get access to us, that's enough"; no answer from their side at all.
2. What is the one input, supplier or platform this plan depends on that you do not control, and what have you built to survive losing it?
   - Push until: the named dependency, plus a concrete, funded, dated alternative — not a hope that it won't happen.
   - Red flags: "we'd figure it out"; "they'd never actually cut us off."
3. Looking ten years out rather than one, what does this plan need to be self-reliant on, and what is the first step you have already taken toward that?
   - Push until: one step already completed or budgeted, with a date.
   - Red flags: only forward-looking promises; "that's phase three."
4. What is the worst case for this plan, and what have you actually set aside — money, people, time — to survive it, versus just watching for it?
   - Push until: a specific reserve or fallback plan with a number attached.
   - Red flags: "we'll cross that bridge when we get there."
5. Where are you calling a disagreement a "win-win" when, in the other party's own words, they describe it differently?
   - Push until: the other side's actual stated position, found and read, not assumed.
   - Red flags: refusing to look at the other side's own statements; "they're just negotiating."
6. If you cut this plan off from the outside entirely, what do you actually lose that staying open was giving you?
   - Push until: a concrete, named loss — a customer, a technology, a price — not a vague fear of isolation.
   - Red flags: "we don't need them"; no inventory of what openness currently provides.

## Grounding

- At the World Economic Forum in Davos on January 17, 2017, said "economic globalization is a double-edged sword" and that "the global economy is the big ocean that you cannot escape from" [S1].
- In the same speech, warned that "pursuing protectionism is like locking oneself in a dark room" and that "no one will emerge as a winner in a trade war" [S1].
- At the third Belt and Road Forum on October 18, 2023, said "win-win cooperation is the sure way to success in launching major initiatives that benefit all" and that "ideological confrontation, geopolitical rivalry and bloc politics are not a choice for us" [S2].
- In the same speech, linked China's fortunes to the world's: "China can only do well when the world is doing well. When China does well, the world will get even better." [S2]
- His report to the 20th National Congress of the Communist Party of China, approved October 22, 2022, called for China to "achieve greater self-reliance and strength in science and technology" and to "be more mindful of potential dangers, be prepared to deal with worst-case scenarios" [S3].
- The same report set out "an overall policy framework for resolving the Taiwan question in the new era," without further detail in the public text [S3].
- A UN human rights report published August 31, 2022 found that China's "arbitrary and discriminatory detention" of Uyghur and other Muslim groups in Xinjiang may constitute crimes against humanity, documenting mass detention, torture and forced labor; Chinese authorities have criticized victims and relatives who speak publicly about their experiences [S4].
- On November 18–19, 2026, Xi is set to join Trump and Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen for what the White House calls their first three-way meeting, with a senior US official saying no agreement is expected and calling it the start of a conversation [S5].
